SpatialEconometrics.jl

Spatial and structural econometrics in Julia — cross-section, panel and higher-order models, diagnostics and weight-matrix construction, built on sparse linear algebra, multiple dispatch and automatic differentiation.

v0.3.0 27 submodules 3,415 tests Julia 1.10+ MIT

What's here

Cross-sectional models

  • SAR, SDM, SEM by concentrated ML
  • SLX, SAC/SARAR, SDEM — separate lag and error weights
  • Exact LU or Chebyshev log-determinant
  • spatial_effects: direct, indirect, total

Panel models

  • Static spatial panels, FE/RE, Lee–Yu bias correction
  • Dynamic panels by Direct QML
  • GS2SLS / SARAR panels with endogenous regressors
  • Arellano–Bond and Blundell–Bond system GMM

Heterogeneity

  • GWR and MGWR, adaptive or fixed bandwidth
  • Spatial regimes with SKATER regionalization
  • Spatial probit, ordered probit, marginal effects
  • Eigenvector spatial filtering and RE-ESF

Flows and calibration

  • Gravity models: PPML and negative binomial
  • Origin, destination and bilateral fixed effects
  • Multilateral resistance terms
  • GRAS balancing and Leontief multipliers

Diagnostics

  • Moran's I (global and local), Geary's C
  • LM battery: lag, error, and both robust forms
  • Koley–Bera Durbin score tests
  • Panel Hausman, Chow, joint LM, panel Moran

Inference and output

  • Driscoll–Kraay SCC and Conley spatial HAC
  • AIC/BIC, LR, Vuong, common-factor and Wald tests
  • Coefficient and side-by-side regression tables
  • LaTeX, CSV and Markdown export

Two optional extensions load themselves when their packages are present: Turing.jl for Bayesian spatial models, and Makie.jl for LISA maps and coefficient surfaces. See Package Extensions.

Installation

using Pkg
Pkg.add(url = "https://github.com/randsley/SpatialEconometrics.jl")

A worked example

Simulate a lag process on a 15×15 rook lattice, then work the way you would with real data: ask what kind of dependence is there, fit the specification the tests point to, and read off the effects.

using SpatialEconometrics, Random

W = row_standardize(make_rook_weights(15))   # 15×15 lattice → 225 units
X = reshape(randn(MersenneTwister(20261030), 225), 225, 1)
y, _ = simulate_spatial(SAR_DGP(0.6, [2.0], 1.0), W, X; rng = MersenneTwister(1))

Which dependence? The LM battery separates a lag process from an error process. The two robust statistics are the ones to read: each conditions on the other form of dependence, so only one of them should fire.

lt = lm_tests(y, hcat(ones(225), X), W)

print(lt.lag)
print(lt.error)
print(lt.robust_lag)
print(lt.robust_error)
LM-Lag: stat = 144.8974    p = 0.0000 ***
LM-Error: stat = 56.4453    p = 0.0000 ***
Robust LM-Lag: stat = 89.4546    p = 0.0000 ***
Robust LM-Error: stat = 1.0024    p = 0.3167

The robust lag test is decisive and the robust error test is not, which is the signature of a lag process — correctly, since that is how the data were generated.

Fit it.

m = fit_spatial(SAR(), W, X, y; varnames = ["income"])
coeftable(m)

Read the effects. With ρ ≠ 0 a coefficient is not a marginal effect: a change in one unit's x propagates through the lattice and back. The direct, indirect and total decomposition is what you report.

spatial_effects(m)

Choosing a model

If the dependence is in…the model isentry point
the outcome, y = ρWy + Xβ + εSARfit_spatial(SAR(), …)
the disturbance, u = λWu + εSEMfit_spatial_error(…)
the outcome and the covariatesSDMfit_spatial(SDM(), …)
the covariates aloneSLXfit_slx(…)
the outcome and the disturbanceSAC / SARARfit_sac(…)
the covariates and the disturbanceSDEMfit_sdem(…)
coefficients that vary over spaceGWR / MGWRfit_gwr(…), fit_mgwr(…)
coefficients that vary by regimeregimesfit_spatial_regime(…)
