Calibration & Input-Output

Balancing a matrix to new row and column margins while preserving as much of its structure as possible — the standard problem in updating a social accounting matrix or a multi-regional input-output table to a new year's totals.

GRAS

The generalized RAS algorithm. Unlike plain RAS it handles negative entries, which matter because a SAM legitimately contains them (inventory changes, net taxes), and a sign-blind scaling would destroy them.

# Full result with convergence information
res = balance_gras_full(X0, u_target, v_target; tol = 1e-11)
# res.X_balanced, res.converged, res.kl_divergence, res.iterations

# Simple interface, differentiable via ChainRulesCore + ForwardDiff
X_bal = balance_gras(X0, u_target, v_target)

u_target is the vector of required row sums and v_target the required column sums; they must agree in total, since no scaling can satisfy inconsistent margins. Prefer balance_gras_full when the result feeds a decision: it reports whether the iteration actually converged and how far the balanced matrix moved from the prior, rather than silently returning the last iterate.

balance_gras is differentiable, so a balancing step can sit inside a larger calibration or an automatic-differentiation pipeline.

Generalized cross-entropy

When there is information beyond the margins — a known subtotal, an estimated coefficient, a bound — cross-entropy minimization imposes it as a side constraint with an uncertainty on each:

X_bal, λ_dual, μ_dual = balance_generalized_ce(X0, G, b, sigma_b)

G and b state the constraints as G * vec(X) = b, and sigma_b gives each one a standard deviation, so a constraint known loosely is not imposed as though it were exact. The duals say which constraints are doing the work.

SAM multipliers

M, A = sam_leontief_multipliers(X_sam, endogenous_idx)

endogenous_idx selects the accounts treated as endogenous; A is the resulting coefficient matrix and M the Leontief inverse. Which accounts are endogenous is a modelling decision, not a detail — it determines what the multipliers mean.
