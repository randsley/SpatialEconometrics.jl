Root Estimator of ρ

API reference for RootRho. See Root Estimator of ρ for the discussion: the moment, the two intervals, how to choose the basis or the penalty, and what the Monte Carlo measured.

Module

SpatialEconometrics.RootRho — Module
RootRho

Jin & Lee's (2012) root estimator of ρ in a spatial autoregression, with a first stage that projects on a basis whose size may grow with n.

\[y = ρWy + Xβ + f + ε\]

f is a smooth nuisance approximated in the span of a basis B that the user supplies (a tensor spline or hat basis on the locations, for example). The estimator needs no log-determinant: ρ̂ is a root of a quadratic.

The trace correction is what makes this work when B is large. With the projection M = I − P_[X,B], Smirnov's plain closed form has a moment whose mean at the true ρ is of order K/n (K the number of columns of [X, B]), so it is biased, and badly so once a free surface makes K large; closed_form_rho is that estimator. Jin & Lee's moment y₀′Cy₀ uses a C with a zero diagonal and C[X, B] = 0, so its mean is exactly zero whatever the heteroskedasticity.

Inference

Two intervals are reported for ρ:

  • the Wald interval ρ̂ ± z√V̂/|g′(ρ̂)|, with the HC2 variance V̂ that includes a noise term for the estimated P_[X,B]y₀;
  • the test-inversion set CS = {ρ : g(ρ)² ≤ z²V̂(ρ)}, an Anderson–Rubin-type set. It does not need ρ to be identified: where the data say little about ρ, it is wide, and it may reach the bounds, while the Wald interval under-covers. Report it.

With a penalised smoother

Given a penalty and lambda, the first stage is the penalised smoother S = Z(Z′Z + λΩ)⁻¹Z′ (only the basis is penalised), and M = I − S is no longer idempotent. Then the moment is the one-step exact-diagonal moment y₀′Ay₀ with A = MW + MDM, (M∘M)d = −diag(MW), so diag A = 0, and the HC2 plug-in is ûᵢ²/(M²)ᵢᵢ (THEORY.md, Proposition 4). Jin & Lee's two-step is not used there: its plug-in result needs Mf = 0, which a smoother breaks by shrinking f. The smoother's shrinkage biases ρ̂ and both intervals, so smooth less than a good fit of f would. tensor_pspline builds a tensor P-spline basis and penalty.

The growing-basis results (a CLT for the moment with a dense M, the consistency of the HC2 V̂, the validity of CS, and the penalised smoother, and its bias-aware interval) are proved in research/trace_cfe/THEORY.md (Theorem 1, Propositions 1, 1′, 2, 4, 6, 6″, 7 and 7′, and Lemmas 4, 5 and 7–10). They have not been checked by anyone else. They are measured in that directory and in validation/mc_root_rho.jl, validation/mc_root_rho_smoother.jl and validation/mc_root_rho_hc2_worst.jl.

References

  • Jin, F., & Lee, L.-F. (2012). Approximated likelihood and root estimators for spatial interaction in spatial autoregressive models. Regional Science and Urban Economics, 42(3), 446–458.
  • Smirnov, O. A. (2022). A closed-form consistent estimator for linear models with spatial dependence. Geographical Analysis, 54(1), 3–31. https://doi.org/10.1111/gean.12268
  • Anderson, T. W., & Rubin, H. (1949). Estimation of the parameters of a single equation in a complete system of stochastic equations. Annals of Mathematical Statistics, 20(1), 46–63.

Estimation

SpatialEconometrics.RootRho.RootRhoResult — Type
RootRhoResult

The result of root_rho.

Fields

  • rho — the root ρ̂: REsd's with a projection, the exact-diagonal moment's with a penalised smoother (NaN when no admissible root exists; see root_found).
  • rho_first — Jin & Lee's first-step root ρ̃ (NaN if none, and always NaN with a penalised smoother, which takes one step).
  • se — √V̂(ρ̂)/|g′(ρ̂)|, with the HC2 V̂.
  • ci_wald — ρ̂ ± z·se.
  • cs — the test-inversion set, as a vector of closed intervals within rho_bounds. Usually one interval; empty if the moment rejects every ρ in the bounds.
  • cs_touches_bound, cs_split — whether cs reaches a bound, and whether it is more than one interval.
  • level — the confidence level of both.
  • beta — the coefficients of X from least squares of y − ρ̂Wy on [X, B]. No standard errors: the theory covers ρ only.
  • varnames — the names of beta.
  • n, K — the sample size and the number of columns of [X, B].
  • smoother — :projection or :penalised.
  • lambda — the penalty's λ (0 for a projection).
  • df — the effective degrees of freedom tr S of the first stage (K for a projection).
  • max_leverage — maxᵢ Sᵢᵢ of the first stage (hᵢᵢ for a projection).
  • a, b, c — the final moment g(ρ) = c − bρ + aρ².
  • vpoly — V̂(ρ) = Σₖ vpoly[k+1] ρᵏ, a quartic.
  • vhat — V̂(ρ̂).
  • root_found — false when the moment has no admissible root at either step. Then rho, se and ci_wald are NaN, and cs is still valid.
  • rho_bounds.
  • vhat_worst_bias — for a projection, the worst case of the HC2 V̂'s relative bias (E V̂ − V)/V at ρ₀, over symmetric homoskedastic error laws (THEORY.md, Lemma 8). It is computed from W, [X, B] and the final moment matrix alone, and is reached by Rademacher (±σ) errors at f = 0. The test-inversion set then covers at least 2Φ(z√(1 + vhat_worst_bias)) − 1 for large n (Proposition 6″). It is 0 when the bias cannot be negative, -Inf when it has no worst case, and NaN for a penalised smoother, where Lemma 8 does not apply. The HC2 V̂ is not consistent when K/n does not tend to 0, and this is the price.
  • roughness — the bound ℛ given for the bias-aware interval (NaN when none was asked for).
  • bias_bound — b̄, the bound on the moment's bias |E g(ρ₀)| over the roughness class (0 for a projection; NaN when not asked for).
  • ci_bias_aware — ρ̂ ± cv(b̄/√V̂)·se, with cv(t) the level quantile of |N(t, 1)| (NaN when not asked for).
  • lambda_grid, halflength_grid — with a vector of lambdas, every candidate (the projection as λ = 0 first) and the half-length of its bias-aware interval; the result is the shortest. Empty otherwise.
  • reference_K — the number of columns of [X, reference basis] when the roughness class is stated on a reference basis (Lemma 5); 0 otherwise.
  • basis_grid, basis_index — with a vector of bases, the basis of each candidate in lambda_grid (an index into the vector), and the index of the chosen one. Empty and 0 otherwise.

SpatialEconometrics.RootRho.root_rho — Function
root_rho(y, X, W; basis = nothing, penalty = nothing, lambda = nothing,
         roughness = nothing, bonferroni = false, reference = nothing,
         varnames = nothing, level = 0.95, rho_bounds = (-0.99, 0.99),
         max_dense_n = 4000)

Jin & Lee's (2012) two-step root estimator of ρ in y = ρWy + Xβ + f + ε, with the first stage a projection on Z = [X, basis]. The moment is

\[g(ρ) = y_0(ρ)'\,C\,y_0(ρ),\quad y_0(ρ) = y − ρWy,\quad C = (A − Δ)M,\; Δ = \mathrm{Diag}(AM)\,\mathrm{Diag}(M)^{-1},\]

with M = I − P_Z, so diag C = 0 and CZ = 0, and E g(ρ₀) = 0 under any heteroskedasticity. The first step uses A = W′ and gives ρ̃; the second (their REsd) uses A = G(ρ̃)′, G(r) = W(I − rW)⁻¹, and gives ρ̂. Each is the root of a quadratic at which g′ < 0.

Two intervals are returned (see RootRhoResult): the Wald interval with the HC2 V̂, and the test-inversion set {ρ : g(ρ)² ≤ z²V̂(ρ)}, which stays valid when ρ is weakly identified. V̂(ρ) is a quartic in ρ, so the set's endpoints are exact.

When K/n is not small. The HC2 V̂ is not consistent when K/n tends to θ > 0. Its relative bias at ρ₀ has an explicit limit (THEORY.md, Lemma 7), and the test-inversion set covers 2Φ(z√(1 + β_V)) − 1 for large n (Proposition 6″). That needs a local basis, one whose projection draws on a bounded total weight of the units (Lemma 10), such as tensor_pspline. With homoskedastic symmetric errors, the bias is at least the worst case over error laws, which is computed from the design alone (Lemma 8) and reported as vhat_worst_bias, for a projection. It is O(K/n) (Lemma 9). It was −0.4% to −2.7% at K/n = 0.06–0.40 in the designs measured, so the set's coverage stays at or above about 94.7%. With heteroskedastic or skewed errors the bias has no such design-only bound. It was −1% to +20% where measured. An adversarial search over variance patterns gives a worse case: with the largest variance up to 4 times the smallest, it found −4% to −12% (3 to 6 times vhat_worst_bias), and up to 10 times, −8% to −21%. The set still covered 0.937–0.953 at those patterns (research/tracecfe/ tchc2hetero.jl). So `vhatworst_biasis the worst case only when all units share one error variance. The computation adds twon × n × n` products, about 1.8 s at n = 4000.

Arguments

  • X — regressors whose β is reported (point estimates only). May have zero columns. Include the intercept here or in basis, not both: a hat or B-spline basis already sums to one.
  • W — a spatial weights matrix with a zero diagonal, row-standardised so that rho_bounds lies inside (−1, 1).
  • basis — the n × K_B basis for f. With reference and roughness, also a vector of bases, among which the shortest interval chooses (see "Choosing the basis").
  • penalty, lambda — a symmetric positive semi-definite K_B × K_B penalty Ω on the basis coefficients, and its weight λ > 0. Give both or neither. tensor_pspline returns a basis and a penalty. With roughness, lambda may also be a vector (see below). With a vector of bases, penalty is a vector too, one per basis, and lambda applies to each.
  • roughness — a bound ℛ on the roughness √(ψ′Πψ) of f = [X, basis]ψ (Π the penalty on the basis block), for the bias-aware interval. basis_roughness computes it from a reference surface.
  • bonferroni — with a vector of lambdas or of bases, use level 1 − α/L for each of the L candidates, so that coverage holds for any choice among them.
  • reference — a finer basis and penalty (; basis, penalty), as tensor_pspline returns, on which roughness states the class, so that f need not lie in span(basis) (see below). Then roughness may be given without penalty, for the projection alone.

With a penalised smoother

The first stage is S = Z(Z′Z + λΩ̃)⁻¹Z′, where Ω̃ applies Ω to the basis and leaves X unpenalised. M = I − S is not idempotent, so a different moment is used, in one step:

\[g(ρ) = y_0(ρ)'\,A\,y_0(ρ),\quad A = MW + MDM,\quad (M∘M)\,d = −\mathrm{diag}(MW),\]

so diag A = 0 and E g(ρ₀) = f′Af, the bias from the smoother's shrinkage of f (THEORY.md, Proposition 4). The HC2 plug-in is ûᵢ²/(M²)ᵢᵢ, and the noise term uses H = (A + A′)S. Jin & Lee's two-step is not used here: its plug-in result needs Mf = 0.

Choose a small λ. The shrinkage bias grows with λ, and it makes both intervals under-cover. GCV and the RMSE-optimal λ were too large for valid inference in research/trace_cfe/tc_lambda.jl, so lambda has no default. Report ρ̂ across a few values of λ, with r.df = tr S. A df/n above 0.30 gives a warning, and units with Sᵢᵢ above 0.99 are refused.

The bias-aware interval

The smoother's shrinkage biases the moment by β = f′Af, which the Wald interval and the test-inversion set ignore. Given roughness = ℛ, the result also holds the interval

\[ρ̂ ± \mathrm{cv}(\bar b/\sqrt{\hat V})\,\mathrm{se},\qquad \bar b = ℛ\sqrt{v'Π^+v} + ℛ^2\bar χ,\]

where b̄ bounds |β| for every f = [X, basis]ψ with ψ′Πψ ≤ ℛ² (THEORY.md, Lemma 4), v = (MZ)′Wf is estimated from the projection (at Jin & Lee's first-step root, and debiased), χ̄ bounds the quadratic part, and cv(t) is the level quantile of |N(t, 1)|. Its coverage holds uniformly over that class, whatever λ (Proposition 7), so λ no longer has to be small for the interval to be valid. With ℛ = 0 it is the Wald interval.

With a vector of lambdas, each is fitted, the projection is added as λ = 0 (its bias bound is 0), and the result is the candidate with the shortest bias-aware interval; lambda_grid and halflength_grid record them all. Proposition 7 covers that choice when one candidate is clearly the shortest; bonferroni = true covers any choice, at a cost in length (about 46% at b̄ = 0 with 12 candidates).

ℛ is an assumption, not an estimate. The data cannot certify an upper bound on the roughness, and every rule that estimated it under-covered (research/trace_cfe/tc_rchoice.jl). State it through a reference surface at least as rough as f is believed to be (basis_roughness), and err on the large side: a large ℛ moves the choice towards the projection and costs at most the gain, a small one costs coverage. In tc_honest.jl's design (n = 900), with ℛ the true roughness, the chosen interval covered 0.954 and 0.964 and was 53% and 33% shorter than the projection's; with ℛ four times too large, it covered 0.982 and 0.970 and was still 35% and 7% shorter. Report the result across a few ℛ.

With a reference basis. The class above contains only f ∈ span(Z), and gives the projection b̄ = 0. A real f leaves a remainder that biases every candidate, the projection too. reference = (; basis = B̃, penalty = Π̃) states the class on a finer basis, {f = [X, B̃]ψ̃ : ψ̃′Π̃ψ̃ ≤ ℛ̃²}, while the estimator keeps basis. The bound b̃ then covers the remainder (THEORY.md, Lemma 5 and Proposition 7′). It needs the reference's unpenalised functions to be unpenalised in [X, basis] (tensor hat bases on the same coordinates, with the same penalty order), which is checked. State ℛ̃ with basis_roughness(g, u, v; k) for a reference surface g:

ref = tensor_pspline(u, v; k = 30)
ℛ̃ = basis_roughness((s, t) -> 2sin(2πs)cos(πt), u, v; k = 30)
r = root_rho(y, X, W; ps..., lambda = 10.0 .^ (-3:0.5:2), roughness = ℛ̃, reference = ref)

Use it whenever basis may be too coarse for f. In research/trace_cfe/tc_refbasis.jl (n = 900), the class without a reference covered 0.050, 0.717 and 0.923 with 4, 5 and 7 knots a side and a rough f; with the reference, every cell covered 0.960–1.000. The price is length, and it is small only for a fine basis: with 15 knots and ℛ̃ true, 0% and 8% longer than without; with ℛ̃ four times too large, 7% and 115%; with coarse bases, 1.1 to 10 times. So the projection is no longer free of the bound, and with a reference the rule can do worse than the projection's Wald interval, which is then not valid. Because the class no longer depends on basis, results across bases are comparable, and the shortest interval was at the finest basis tried. The projection candidate is REsd, which THEORY.md's Proposition 1′ covers when the largest remainder ‖Mf‖ in the class is small against √n (Proposition 7′(vi)). Each candidate costs two more n × n × K̃ products and a K̃ × K̃ eigenproblem: a grid of 13 took about 3.5 s at n = 900, K̃ = 901.

Choosing the basis

The interval is valid only if the approximation error of f is small against the sampling noise (THEORY.md, Proposition 3: β = o(√n)). That calls for a larger basis than a good fit of f would: GCV and the RMSE-optimal size over-smooth for inference (research/trace_cfe/, tc_lambda.jl). Report ρ̂ across a few basis sizes. The coverage of both intervals was measured for K/n up to 0.30; a larger K gives a warning. Units with leverage above 0.99 make the correction ill-posed and are refused.

Chosen by the data, with a reference basis. Once the class is stated on reference, it does not depend on basis, and the bias-aware intervals of different bases bound the same thing. So basis may be a vector, and the shortest interval over every (basis, λ) pair is returned (THEORY.md, Corollary 7′); basis_grid and basis_index record the choice, and bonferroni = true counts every pair. Each basis needs Lemma 5's condition (R), and costs one fit per λ:

ref = tensor_pspline(u, v; k = 30)
pss = [tensor_pspline(u, v; k) for k in (5, 10, 15)]
r = root_rho(y, X, W; basis = [p.basis for p in pss], penalty = [p.penalty for p in pss],
             lambda = 10.0 .^ (-3:0.5:2), roughness = ℛ̃, reference = ref)

Without a reference this is refused: the class F(ℛ) then depends on the basis, and a coarse basis would look short only because its class is small.

The root rule

g is a quadratic in ρ, and ρ̂ is the root at which g′ < 0. This assumes J = −E g′(ρ₀) > 0 (THEORY.md, A9). If J < 0, the other root is the consistent one, and the rule picks a root at a distance of about |J/g₂| from ρ₀ (g₂ the quadratic coefficient), which does not shrink with n. A 2SLS estimate (instruments [X, basis, W·[X, basis]]) is used to check the sign: if g′ is positive there, a warning is given. The test-inversion set does not use the rule and stays valid.

J < 0 is rare. In a search over 3,000 hub-and-spoke designs, 0.7% had it; the one in testset 47g gives a sign-rule root biased by 0.06 (2 SD) with the smoother. In 1,000 fits on kNN designs (n 400–900, both paths) the check never fired. REsd's second step has J > 0 in the designs measured, even when the first step's J is negative, so on the projection path the warning is rarer still.

Cost

Everything is dense: about four n × n matrices, so n is capped at max_dense_n (4000 by default, about 0.5 GB). The penalised smoother holds about six and solves one dense n × n system for d.

Proved, but not checked by anyone else

The results for a basis that grows with n (a CLT for the moment, the consistency of V̂, the validity of the test-inversion set, and the coverage of the bias-aware interval, also on a reference basis and with the basis chosen by the data) are in research/trace_cfe/THEORY.md. The published theory (Jin & Lee 2012) assumes a design of fixed dimension.

Basis and penalty

SpatialEconometrics.RootRho.tensor_pspline — Function
tensor_pspline(u, v; k, order = 2) -> (; basis, penalty)

A tensor-product P-spline on two coordinates, for the basis and penalty arguments of root_rho:

r = root_rho(y, X, W; tensor_pspline(u, v; k = 15)..., lambda = 1.0)
  • basis — n × k²: the products of piecewise-linear "hat" functions on k equally spaced knots over the range of u and of v. Column (i − 1)k + j is hᵢ(u)·hⱼ(v). The rows sum to one, so do not also put an intercept in X.
  • penalty — k² × k²: P ⊗ I + I ⊗ P with P = D′D and D the order-th difference matrix: a difference penalty along each coordinate. It is symmetric and positive semi-definite. With order = 2 it leaves four functions unpenalised: those that are linear in each coordinate's knot index, 1, i, j, ij.

k must be at least order + 2. Used without penalty, basis is the projection basis of the default root_rho.

SpatialEconometrics.RootRho.basis_roughness — Function
basis_roughness(f_ref, basis, penalty; X = nothing) -> ℛ

The roughness √(ψ′Πψ) of a reference surface f_ref (a vector over the n units) in a basis and penalty: ψ is the least-squares fit of f_ref on [X, basis], and Π applies penalty to the basis block and leaves X unpenalised, as root_rho does. Use it to set roughness: choose an f_ref at least as rough as f is believed to be. The data cannot choose ℛ (research/trace_cfe/THEORY.md, "Choosing ℛ"), and too small an ℛ costs coverage, so err on the large side.

For a tensor hat basis with knot spacing h and a second-difference penalty, ψ′Πψ ≈ h²∫(f_uu² + f_vv²), so ℛ is also a bound on the mean squared curvature (measured ratio 1.10 in tc_rchoice.jl).

basis_roughness(g, u, v; k, order = 2) -> ℛ

The roughness of a reference surface g(u, v) (a function) in tensor_pspline(u, v; k, order): ψ holds g at the knots, in the basis's column order, so Bψ is the hat interpolant of g. Use this form for a reference basis in root_rho: a fine reference has more columns than there are units, and the least-squares fit above is then not defined. As k grows, (k − 1)·ℛ settles (to within 2% between k = 15 and 40 in tc_refbasis.jl), so the class does not depend on the reference grid once it is fine. Unpenalised functions (bilinear surfaces, for order = 2) give 0.
