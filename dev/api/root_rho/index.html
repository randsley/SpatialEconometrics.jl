Root Estimator of ρ

API reference for RootRho. See Root Estimator of ρ for the discussion: the moment, the two intervals, how to choose the basis or the penalty, and what the Monte Carlo measured.

Module

SpatialEconometrics.RootRho — Module
RootRho

Jin & Lee's (2012) root estimator of ρ in a spatial autoregression, with a first stage that projects on a basis whose size may grow with n.

\[y = ρWy + Xβ + f + ε\]

f is a smooth nuisance approximated in the span of a basis B that the user supplies (a tensor spline or hat basis on the locations, for example). The estimator needs no log-determinant: ρ̂ is a root of a quadratic.

The trace correction is what makes this work when B is large. With the projection M = I − P_[X,B], Smirnov's plain closed form has a moment whose mean at the true ρ is of order K/n (K the number of columns of [X, B]), so it is biased, and badly so once a free surface makes K large; closed_form_rho is that estimator. Jin & Lee's moment y₀′Cy₀ uses a C with a zero diagonal and C[X, B] = 0, so its mean is exactly zero whatever the heteroskedasticity.

Inference

Two intervals are reported for ρ:

  • the Wald interval ρ̂ ± z√V̂/|g′(ρ̂)|, with the HC2 variance V̂ that includes a noise term for the estimated P_[X,B]y₀;
  • the test-inversion set CS = {ρ : g(ρ)² ≤ z²V̂(ρ)}, an Anderson–Rubin-type set. It does not need ρ to be identified: where the data say little about ρ, it is wide, and it may reach the bounds, while the Wald interval under-covers. Report it.

With a penalised smoother

Given a penalty and lambda, the first stage is the penalised smoother S = Z(Z′Z + λΩ)⁻¹Z′ (only the basis is penalised), and M = I − S is no longer idempotent. Then the moment is the one-step exact-diagonal moment y₀′Ay₀ with A = MW + MDM, (M∘M)d = −diag(MW), so diag A = 0, and the HC2 plug-in is ûᵢ²/(M²)ᵢᵢ (THEORY.md, Proposition 4). Jin & Lee's two-step is not used there: its plug-in result needs Mf = 0, which a smoother breaks by shrinking f. The smoother's shrinkage biases ρ̂ and both intervals, so smooth less than a good fit of f would. tensor_pspline builds a tensor P-spline basis and penalty.

The growing-basis results (a CLT for the moment with a dense M, the consistency of the HC2 V̂, the validity of CS, and the penalised smoother) are proved in research/trace_cfe/THEORY.md (Theorem 1 and Propositions 1, 2, 4 and 6). They have not been checked by anyone else. They are measured in that directory and in validation/mc_root_rho.jl and validation/mc_root_rho_smoother.jl.

References

  • Jin, F., & Lee, L.-F. (2012). Approximated likelihood and root estimators for spatial interaction in spatial autoregressive models. Regional Science and Urban Economics, 42(3), 446–458.
  • Smirnov, O. A. (2022). A closed-form consistent estimator for linear models with spatial dependence. Geographical Analysis, 54(1), 3–31. https://doi.org/10.1111/gean.12268
  • Anderson, T. W., & Rubin, H. (1949). Estimation of the parameters of a single equation in a complete system of stochastic equations. Annals of Mathematical Statistics, 20(1), 46–63.

Estimation

SpatialEconometrics.RootRho.RootRhoResult — Type
RootRhoResult

The result of root_rho.

Fields

  • rho — the root ρ̂: REsd's with a projection, the exact-diagonal moment's with a penalised smoother (NaN when no admissible root exists; see root_found).
  • rho_first — Jin & Lee's first-step root ρ̃ (NaN if none, and always NaN with a penalised smoother, which takes one step).
  • se — √V̂(ρ̂)/|g′(ρ̂)|, with the HC2 V̂.
  • ci_wald — ρ̂ ± z·se.
  • cs — the test-inversion set, as a vector of closed intervals within rho_bounds. Usually one interval; empty if the moment rejects every ρ in the bounds.
  • cs_touches_bound, cs_split — whether cs reaches a bound, and whether it is more than one interval.
  • level — the confidence level of both.
  • beta — the coefficients of X from least squares of y − ρ̂Wy on [X, B]. No standard errors: the theory covers ρ only.
  • varnames — the names of beta.
  • n, K — the sample size and the number of columns of [X, B].
  • smoother — :projection or :penalised.
  • lambda — the penalty's λ (0 for a projection).
  • df — the effective degrees of freedom tr S of the first stage (K for a projection).
  • max_leverage — maxᵢ Sᵢᵢ of the first stage (hᵢᵢ for a projection).
  • a, b, c — the final moment g(ρ) = c − bρ + aρ².
  • vpoly — V̂(ρ) = Σₖ vpoly[k+1] ρᵏ, a quartic.
  • vhat — V̂(ρ̂).
  • root_found — false when the moment has no admissible root at either step. Then rho, se and ci_wald are NaN, and cs is still valid.
  • rho_bounds.

SpatialEconometrics.RootRho.root_rho — Function
root_rho(y, X, W; basis = nothing, penalty = nothing, lambda = nothing,
         varnames = nothing, level = 0.95, rho_bounds = (-0.99, 0.99),
         max_dense_n = 4000)

Jin & Lee's (2012) two-step root estimator of ρ in y = ρWy + Xβ + f + ε, with the first stage a projection on Z = [X, basis]. The moment is

\[g(ρ) = y_0(ρ)'\,C\,y_0(ρ),\quad y_0(ρ) = y − ρWy,\quad C = (A − Δ)M,\; Δ = \mathrm{Diag}(AM)\,\mathrm{Diag}(M)^{-1},\]

with M = I − P_Z, so diag C = 0 and CZ = 0, and E g(ρ₀) = 0 under any heteroskedasticity. The first step uses A = W′ and gives ρ̃; the second (their REsd) uses A = G(ρ̃)′, G(r) = W(I − rW)⁻¹, and gives ρ̂. Each is the root of a quadratic at which g′ < 0.

Two intervals are returned (see RootRhoResult): the Wald interval with the HC2 V̂, and the test-inversion set {ρ : g(ρ)² ≤ z²V̂(ρ)}, which stays valid when ρ is weakly identified. V̂(ρ) is a quartic in ρ, so the set's endpoints are exact.

Arguments

  • X — regressors whose β is reported (point estimates only). May have zero columns. Include the intercept here or in basis, not both: a hat or B-spline basis already sums to one.
  • W — a spatial weights matrix with a zero diagonal, row-standardised so that rho_bounds lies inside (−1, 1).
  • basis — the n × K_B basis for f.
  • penalty, lambda — a symmetric positive semi-definite K_B × K_B penalty Ω on the basis coefficients, and its weight λ > 0. Give both or neither. tensor_pspline returns a basis and a penalty.

With a penalised smoother

The first stage is S = Z(Z′Z + λΩ̃)⁻¹Z′, where Ω̃ applies Ω to the basis and leaves X unpenalised. M = I − S is not idempotent, so a different moment is used, in one step:

\[g(ρ) = y_0(ρ)'\,A\,y_0(ρ),\quad A = MW + MDM,\quad (M∘M)\,d = −\mathrm{diag}(MW),\]

so diag A = 0 and E g(ρ₀) = f′Af, the bias from the smoother's shrinkage of f (THEORY.md, Proposition 4). The HC2 plug-in is ûᵢ²/(M²)ᵢᵢ, and the noise term uses H = (A + A′)S. Jin & Lee's two-step is not used here: its plug-in result needs Mf = 0.

Choose a small λ. The shrinkage bias grows with λ, and it makes both intervals under-cover. GCV and the RMSE-optimal λ were too large for valid inference in research/trace_cfe/tc_lambda.jl, so lambda has no default. Report ρ̂ across a few values of λ, with r.df = tr S. A df/n above 0.30 gives a warning, and units with Sᵢᵢ above 0.99 are refused.

Choosing the basis

The interval is valid only if the approximation error of f is small against the sampling noise (THEORY.md, Proposition 3: β = o(√n)). That calls for a larger basis than a good fit of f would: GCV and the RMSE-optimal size over-smooth for inference (research/trace_cfe/, tc_lambda.jl). Report ρ̂ across a few basis sizes. The coverage of both intervals was measured for K/n up to 0.30; a larger K gives a warning. Units with leverage above 0.99 make the correction ill-posed and are refused.

The root rule

g is a quadratic in ρ, and ρ̂ is the root at which g′ < 0. This assumes J = −E g′(ρ₀) > 0 (THEORY.md, A9). If J < 0, the other root is the consistent one, and the rule picks a root at a distance of about |J/g₂| from ρ₀ (g₂ the quadratic coefficient), which does not shrink with n. A 2SLS estimate (instruments [X, basis, W·[X, basis]]) is used to check the sign: if g′ is positive there, a warning is given. The test-inversion set does not use the rule and stays valid.

J < 0 is rare. In a search over 3,000 hub-and-spoke designs, 0.7% had it; the one in testset 47g gives a sign-rule root biased by 0.06 (2 SD) with the smoother. In 1,000 fits on kNN designs (n 400–900, both paths) the check never fired. REsd's second step has J > 0 in the designs measured, even when the first step's J is negative, so on the projection path the warning is rarer still.

Cost

Everything is dense: about four n × n matrices, so n is capped at max_dense_n (4000 by default, about 0.5 GB). The penalised smoother holds about six and solves one dense n × n system for d.

Proved, but not checked by anyone else

The results for a basis that grows with n (a CLT for the moment, the consistency of V̂, the validity of the test-inversion set) are in research/trace_cfe/THEORY.md. The published theory (Jin & Lee 2012) assumes a design of fixed dimension.

Basis and penalty

SpatialEconometrics.RootRho.tensor_pspline — Function
tensor_pspline(u, v; k, order = 2) -> (; basis, penalty)

A tensor-product P-spline on two coordinates, for the basis and penalty arguments of root_rho:

r = root_rho(y, X, W; tensor_pspline(u, v; k = 15)..., lambda = 1.0)
  • basis — n × k²: the products of piecewise-linear "hat" functions on k equally spaced knots over the range of u and of v. Column (i − 1)k + j is hᵢ(u)·hⱼ(v). The rows sum to one, so do not also put an intercept in X.
  • penalty — k² × k²: P ⊗ I + I ⊗ P with P = D′D and D the order-th difference matrix: a difference penalty along each coordinate. It is symmetric and positive semi-definite. With order = 2 it leaves four functions unpenalised: those that are linear in each coordinate's knot index, 1, i, j, ij.

k must be at least order + 2. Used without penalty, basis is the projection basis of the default root_rho.
