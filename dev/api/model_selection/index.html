Model Selection

Information criteria and tests for choosing between specifications, posterior model probabilities over specifications and weight matrices, and selection of k for a k-nearest-neighbour W.

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialModelSelection.CommonFactorTestResult — Type
CommonFactorTestResult

Test of the common-factor restriction, returned by common_factor_test.

Fields: stat, df, p_value, reject.

A Spatial Durbin model reduces to a spatial error model exactly when theta = -rho * beta. Failing to reject means the simpler error specification is adequate; rejecting means the Durbin terms carry information the error model cannot represent.

See also SDM, fit_spatial_error.

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialModelSelection.KnnSelectionResult — Type
KnnSelectionResult

The result of select_knn_w: one fit per candidate k, and the W that won.

Fields

  • k_values: the candidate ks actually fitted, in ascending order. Values of the requested range outside 1 <= k < N are dropped.
  • aic, bic, loglik, rho: one entry per candidate, aligned with k_values. NaN where the fit failed.
  • converged: false where the fit threw, or where rho landed on the edge of rho_bounds and is therefore not an interior maximum.
  • n_islands: isolated units in each candidate W. Zero throughout for knn by construction — every unit has exactly k neighbours — and reported as a check on that rather than because it is expected to fire.
  • best_k, best_W: the minimiser over the converged candidates, and its weight matrix, ready to pass to a fit.
  • criterion: :aic or :bic, whichever was minimised.

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialModelSelection.LRTestResult — Type
LRTestResult

Likelihood-ratio test between nested models, returned by lr_test.

Fields: stat, df, p_value, and both log-likelihoods (ll_restricted, ll_unrestricted).

Warning

Valid only when one model genuinely nests the other and both were fitted to the same data by maximum likelihood. For non-nested comparisons use vuong_test.

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialModelSelection.SpecEvidence — Type
SpecEvidence

Posterior model probabilities over specifications and weight matrices, as returned by bayes_spec_probs.

Fields

  • specs: the specifications compared, in row order.
  • w_names: the weight matrices, in column order.
  • log_marginal: length(specs) × length(w_names) log marginal likelihoods.
  • prob: posterior probabilities over the whole grid; sums to 1.
  • prob_spec, prob_w: the row and column margins of prob.
  • best: (spec, w_name) of the highest posterior cell.
  • prob_global, prob_local: the probability mass on global-spillover specifications (:sar, :sdm) and on local ones (:sem, :sdem, :slx). :ols counts as neither.

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialModelSelection.SpecRoadmapResult — Type
SpecRoadmapResult

The per-W outcome of sdem_roadmap: which of the local-spillover specifications wins on each weight matrix, and by how much.

Fields

  • w_names, specs: the grid searched.
  • criterion: :marginal, :aic or :bic.
  • score: length(w_names) × length(specs) criterion values. Higher is better for :marginal, lower for :aic and :bic.
  • best_spec: the winning specification on each W.
  • margin: the gap to the runner-up on that W, in the units of the criterion. A small margin is the signal that the search has not settled.
  • best_w, best_overall: the winning cell of the whole grid.

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialModelSelection.VuongTestResult — Type
VuongTestResult

Vuong test between two non-nested models, returned by vuong_test.

Fields: stat, p_value, aic_correction, and preference, which is :model1, :model2 or :indistinguishable.

Use this where lr_test does not apply — comparing a spatial lag against a spatial error specification, for instance, since neither nests the other. :indistinguishable is a genuine outcome, not a failure: the data may simply not separate the two.

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialModelSelection.bayes_spec_probs — Function
bayes_spec_probs(y, X, Ws; specs, w_names, kwargs...)

Posterior probabilities over spatial specifications and weight matrices, computed from the log marginal likelihoods of every combination.

This is the global-versus-local spillover question of LeSage (2014) put jointly with the choice of W: with equal prior weight on every cell of the specs × Ws grid, the posterior is

P(spec, W) ∝ exp(log p(y | spec, W))

normalised over the grid, with the maximum subtracted before exponentiating. prob_spec and prob_w are its margins, so a comparison that is decisive about the spillover structure while being indifferent between weight matrices — or the other way round — is visible rather than hidden in a single winner.

Ws is a vector of weight matrices; pass one to compare specifications alone. specs defaults to [:ols, :slx, :sar, :sdm, :sem, :sdem].

Keyword arguments other than specs and w_names are passed through to log_marginal_likelihood, including the prior, which is worth reading about there before reporting a number from here.

References

LeSage, J. P. (2014). Spatial Statistics, 9, 122–145.

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialModelSelection.common_factor_test — Function
common_factor_test(coef_beta, coef_theta, rho, vcov_full)

Tests whether SDM simplifies to SEM, i.e. H₀: θ + ρβ = 0.

  • coef_beta: vector of K slope coefficients (excluding intercept)
  • coef_theta: vector of K spatial lag coefficients (WX terms)
  • rho: spatial autoregressive parameter
  • vcov_full: full variance-covariance matrix of [β; θ; ρ]

Uses the Wald test formulation from Burridge (1981).

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialModelSelection.log_marginal_likelihood — Function
log_marginal_likelihood(spec, y, X, W; kwargs...)

Log marginal likelihood of a spatial specification, with β and σ² integrated out analytically and the spatial parameter integrated numerically.

spec is one of :ols, :slx, :sar, :sdm, :sem, :sdem. The design is [1 X], or [1 X WX] for the three Durbin specifications; :sar and :sdm put the spatial parameter on the response — global spillovers — :sem and :sdem put it on the disturbance — local spillovers — and :ols and :slx have none, so their marginal is closed form and no quadrature runs.

Under the improper prior p(β, σ²) ∝ 1/σ² the inner integral is exact:

p(y | ρ) = |A(ρ)| · π^(-(n-k)/2) · |Z'Z|^(-1/2) · Γ((n-k)/2) · S(ρ)^(-(n-k)/2)

with A(ρ) = I - ρW for a lag model and I - λW for an error model, and S the residual sum of squares at that value. For an error model both S and |Z'Z| are evaluated on the filtered data (I - λW)[y Z], so the determinant term moves with λ. Dropping it is an easy mistake — it is constant for a lag model — and it biases the comparison toward the error specifications.

The outer integral is a trapezoid rule on n_grid points of rho_bounds, carried out by log-sum-exp so the integrand's scale cannot overflow.

Keywords

  • rho_bounds: the support of the prior, (-0.995, 0.995) by default, which is the stationary interval for a row-standardised W.
  • prior: :uniform (default) or :beta, a Beta(beta_shape, beta_shape) density stretched over rho_bounds.
  • n_grid: quadrature nodes, 200 by default.
  • method: ExactLU() or ChebyshevApprox() for log|A|. Exact at or below max_exact_n observations, Chebyshev above it, by default.
  • varnames: covariate names, used in the message if the design turns out to be rank deficient.
The prior matters

A marginal likelihood is not a likelihood ratio: the prior on the spatial parameter does not wash out with sample size, because it is a prior over a parameter the :ols and :slx specifications do not have. Widening rho_bounds spreads that prior thinner and penalises the spatial specifications. Report the setting alongside the probabilities, and if a comparison is close, check it under both priors.

References

LeSage, J. P. (2014). Spatial econometric panel data model specification: A Bayesian approach. Spatial Statistics, 9, 122–145.

LeSage, J. P. & Pace, R. K. (2009). Introduction to Spatial Econometrics. CRC Press, chapter 5.

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialModelSelection.sdem_roadmap — Function
sdem_roadmap(y, X, Ws; criterion = :marginal, w_names, specs, kwargs...)

The Sutton & Sutton (2023) specification search for the local-spillover case: for each candidate weight matrix, decide which of the local specifications fits best, then read the winner across weight matrices.

The default specs are the three the roadmap is about — :sdem (their SDE, Durbin terms with a spatial error), :slx (the same without the error term), and :sem (the error term without the Durbin terms). Pass others to widen the search; :sar and :sdm put the dependence on the response instead, which is the global-spillover case bayes_spec_probs is for.

criterion is :marginal (log marginal likelihood, higher is better), :aic or :bic (lower is better). The marginal likelihood is the default because it is the only one of the three that integrates over the spatial parameter rather than plugging in an estimate, and the specifications being compared differ in whether they have that parameter.

margin[i] reports the gap to the runner-up on weight matrix i. The point of the roadmap is that the choice of W and the choice of specification are decided together; a margin near zero says the data do not separate them, and should be reported rather than resolved by taking the maximum.

References

Sutton, J. & Sutton, J. (2023). A road map to capture the spatial dependence underlying regions' economic resilience. Spatial Economic Analysis, 19(1), 57–72.

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialModelSelection.select_knn_w — Function
select_knn_w(coords, y, X; variant = SAR(), k_range = 2:50, criterion = :aic, ...)

Choose k for a k-nearest-neighbour weight matrix by refitting the model at every candidate k and minimising an information criterion.

coords is N × D positions, and y and X are the same data for every candidate — which is what makes the criteria comparable. X must not include an intercept; fit_spatial adds one.

Returns a KnnSelectionResult, whose best_W can be passed straight to a fit.

What the curve looks like

The criterion is not monotone in k, which is the whole reason to search: a W that is too sparse misses real neighbours, and one that is too dense dilutes them, so the curve falls and then rises. The minimum is usually shallow. Read aic (or bic) as a curve and not only as an argmin — if the spread across the range is a point or two, the data has not identified k and the sensitivity of whatever you conclude to k should be reported.

Convergence

A candidate is marked not converged if its fit throws, or if rho lands on the edge of rho_bounds, where the concentrated likelihood has no interior maximum and its value is not comparable with the others. Non-converged candidates keep their place in the output with NaN criteria and are excluded from the argmin, so a failed fit cannot win by having no number.

Keywords

  • variant = SAR(): any SpatialVariant; SDM() lags X as well, and the lagged regressors then change with k along with W.
  • k_range = 2:50: candidates. Values outside 1 <= k < N are dropped, and an empty intersection is an error.
  • criterion = :aic: or :bic.
  • binary = false, symmetrize = false: passed to knn_weights. Row standardisation is always on, since rho is only interpretable on (-1, 1) with a row-standardised W.
  • any remaining keywords go to fit_spatial, e.g. method = ExactLU().

Examples

sel = select_knn_w(coords, y, X; k_range = 2:25)
println(sel)                                   # the curve's shape and winner
m = fit_spatial(SAR(), sel.best_W, X, y)       # refit on the chosen W

Cost

One full fit per candidate, so length(k_range) fits. Each is cheap while N stays under fit_spatial's max_exact_n (1000 by default), where the trace powers are computed exactly – about 0.016s at N = 200 and 0.18s at N = 600 on one core, so a 2:50 search costs seconds. Above max_exact_n the traces switch to Hutchinson probes and the per-fit cost rises sharply; narrow k_range or raise max_exact_n deliberately rather than by accident.

Measured behaviour

The design: coordinates uniform on the unit square, y from a SAR with rho = 0.5 on the knn W at k = 6, two covariates.

How often the search recovers the generating k, 300 draws, k searched over 2:20 so 5.3% by chance:

Well targeted, not sharp: at N = 100 the exact k is missed more often than it is found, which is the reason KnnSelectionResult returns every candidate's criterion rather than only the winner.

Too sparse understates the dependence, too dense overstates it, and the drift is monotone in k on both sides. Selecting leaves rho-hat close to unbiased at the cost of a little variance against an oracle that knew k (rmse 0.052 against 0.041). Paired against a fixed choice, selection gives the smaller |rho-hat - rho| in 98.3% of draws at k = 2, 69.0% at k = 10 and 93.0% at k = 20.

Note that the bias range here is wider than the "up to 20%" usually quoted for this effect — but it is a property of the range searched, not of the method: k = 20 is simply further from k = 6 than most applications would stray.
