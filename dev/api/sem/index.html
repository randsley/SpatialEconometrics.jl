Spatial Error Models

Models placing the dependence in the disturbance rather than in y.

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialErrorModel.SpatialErrorPanelModel — Type
SpatialErrorPanelModel

A fitted spatial error panel, returned by the panel method of fit_spatial_error.

As SpatialErrorRegressionModel, with the fixed-effects specification and the panel dimensions N and T_per recorded alongside.

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialErrorModel.SpatialErrorRegressionModel — Type
SpatialErrorRegressionModel

A fitted cross-sectional spatial error model, returned by fit_spatial_error. Implements the StatsAPI.RegressionModel interface.

Carries coef and vcov for the regression parameters, the spatial error parameter lambda with its standard error, sigma2, loglik, the weight matrix, residuals and coefnames.

Note

Unlike SpatialRegressionModel, coef is interpretable directly: dependence sits in the error, so there is no feedback through y and no need for spatial_effects. What the model buys you is correct standard errors, not different point estimates.

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialErrorModel.fit_spatial_error — Function
fit_spatial_error(W, X, y; method, varnames, lambda_bounds)
fit_spatial_error(fe_spec, W, X, y, N, T_per; method, varnames, lambda_bounds)

Fit a spatial error model by concentrated maximum likelihood.

\[y = X*beta + u, u = lambda*W*u + e\]

Dependence is in the disturbance rather than in y. OLS stays unbiased under this process but its standard errors are wrong, so the gain is inferential: beta is interpretable as it stands, with no spillover decomposition needed.

The likelihood is concentrated by spatially filtering y and X with (I - lambda*W) at each trial lambda, then maximizing over lambda alone.

The first method fits a cross-section; the second fits a panel, taking a FixedEffectsSpec and the panel dimensions, with X and y stacked units-fastest.

Arguments

  • W: N x N sparse weight matrix.
  • X: covariates without an intercept — one is added.
  • y: response.

Keywords

  • method: a LogDetMethod.
  • varnames: covariate names.
  • lambda_bounds: search interval for lambda, default (-0.995, 0.995).
  • estimator (cross-section only): :ml (default), Gaussian maximum likelihood, or :gmm, Kelejian & Prucha's (2010) generalized moments estimator, which stays consistent, with valid standard errors, when the error variance differs across units. See "Heteroskedasticity" below.

Returns

A SpatialErrorRegressionModel or SpatialErrorPanelModel.

Examples

m = fit_spatial_error(W, X, y; varnames = ["income", "density"])
m.lambda
coef(m)                              # directly interpretable

fit_spatial_error(TwoWayFE(), W, X, y, N, T_per)

Use lm_tests to decide between this and fit_spatial before fitting either.

Heteroskedasticity: estimator = :gmm

Maximum likelihood assumes every unit's error has the same variance, and when it does not, λ̂ can be inconsistent and the information-matrix standard errors are wrong (Arraiz, Drukker, Kelejian & Prucha 2010). estimator = :gmm is Kelejian & Prucha's (2010) estimator for exogenous X. λ comes from the two moment conditions

E[ε′(W′W − diag(W′W))ε] = 0,    E[ε′Wε] = 0,

which hold whatever the variances, because both matrices have a zero diagonal (so W must have one). β is then OLS on the filtered data (feasible GLS), and λ is re-estimated from those residuals, as in their two-step procedure. The covariance of β is the heteroskedasticity-robust jackknife (HC3) sandwich of the filtered regression, which allows for high-leverage units; λ's comes from their eq. (16). With exogenous X the two are asymptotically independent. There is no likelihood: loglik is NaN and sigma2 is the mean squared filtered residual.

The price is efficiency when the variance is in fact constant: Arraiz et al. report root mean square errors of λ̂ about 5–8% above ML's there, in a model with a spatial lag of y as well. In this package's study of the pure error model (validation/mc_spatial_did_gmm.jl, part C: 500 points on an irregular map, a binary distance band, 2,000 replications) the gap was at most 1%. With the error sd proportional to each unit's number of neighbours, ML's λ̂ was biased and its interval under-covered (0.733 for λ = 0.8, coverage 0.745; 0.415 for 0.5, 0.821) where GMM's was not (0.783, 0.950; 0.480, 0.948). β's intervals covered under both.

m = fit_spatial_error(W, X, y; estimator = :gmm)
Identification

The design has to have full column rank, and the matrix checked is the within-transformed one, since that is what gets solved. Under IndivFE a regressor that does not vary over time is swept out entirely, and under TimeFE one that does not vary across units is; neither coefficient can be estimated from the within variation, and both now throw an ArgumentError naming the column instead of returning a number. The same applies to any covariate that is a linear combination of the others.

In the cross-sectional method there is no transform, and the check is on [1 X] as passed.
