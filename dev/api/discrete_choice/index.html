Spatial Discrete Choice

Spatial probit and ordered probit, by linearized GMM and by Gibbs sampling.

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialDiscreteChoice.BayesianSpatialProbitModel — Type
BayesianSpatialProbitModel

A spatial probit estimated by Albert-Chib Gibbs sampling, returned by fit_bayesian_spatial_probit.

Data augmentation draws the latent utilities, which makes the conditional posteriors tractable and avoids the intractable likelihood. Slower than fit_spatial_probit but markedly more precise on rho in small samples, and it carries an intercept.

This is built in, not part of the Turing extension.

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialDiscreteChoice.SpatialOrderedProbitModel — Type
SpatialOrderedProbitModel

A spatial ordered probit, returned by fit_spatial_ordered_probit, for an outcome with J ordered categories.

Alongside the coefficients it carries the cut points separating the categories, drawn from their uniform conditionals between adjacent order statistics of the latent vector.

With J = 2 this reduces to binary probit, which fit_bayesian_spatial_probit handles better since it carries an intercept.

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialDiscreteChoice.SpatialProbitEffects — Type
SpatialProbitEffects

Direct, indirect and total marginal effects on the probability scale for a spatial discrete-choice model, returned by marginal_effects.

Two things compound here: the probit link makes the effect depend on where each unit sits on the curve, and the spatial lag propagates a change through (I - rho*W)^{-1}. The reported effects average over both.

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialDiscreteChoice.SpatialProbitModel — Type
SpatialProbitModel

A spatial probit fitted by linearized GMM, returned by fit_spatial_probit.

Fast, because it avoids the N-dimensional integral the exact likelihood needs, but less precise in small samples than the Gibbs sampler — see fit_bayesian_spatial_probit when precision on rho matters.

Use marginal_effects to interpret the fit: as in any probit the coefficients are not marginal effects, and the spatial lag adds feedback on top of that.

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialDiscreteChoice.fit_bayesian_spatial_probit — Function
fit_bayesian_spatial_probit(W, X, y; n_samples=2000, burnin=500, rng=...)

Fit the spatial autoregressive probit by Gibbs sampling with Albert & Chib (1993) data augmentation, following LeSage & Pace (2009, Ch. 10).

Each sweep: draw the latent y* one site at a time from its truncated conditional, draw β from its conjugate normal conditional, then update ρ by random-walk Metropolis on log|I-ρW| - ½(Ay*-Xβ)'(Ay*-Xβ) with a Uniform(-1,1) prior. The error variance is fixed at 1 for identification.

beta_prior_var sets an isotropic N(0, v·I) prior on β. Reported coef and vcov are the posterior mean and covariance; accept_rate is the ρ acceptance rate, which should land roughly in 0.2–0.5. The default rho_proposal_sd of 0.2 targets that band; 0.05 overshoots to about 0.77 acceptance and mixes slowly.

Because the observed signs pin the latent vector only up to scale, ‖β‖ and ρ trade off along a weakly identified ridge, and at small N the posterior is not centred on the truth. Over 40 replications on rook lattices at ρ = 0.4, with the chain at its default length:

Nρ biasβ₁ biasρ coverageβ₁ coverage
36−0.161+37.9%87.5%97.5%
64−0.160+23.6%80.0%92.5%
144−0.059+5.5%90.0%92.5%
256−0.025+3.1%92.5%97.5%

β₁ bias is a percentage of its true value. The Monte Carlo standard error on the ρ biases is 0.045 at N = 36 falling to 0.018 at N = 256, so the first two are well clear of zero and the last is marginal. ρ is pulled toward zero and ‖β‖ pushed out together — that pairing is the ridge — and both decay with N.

This is a property of the posterior at small N, not a mixing failure, so a longer chain does not remove it. At N = 64, over 30 replications on identical data, going from 1500 draws to 20000 raised the effective sample size for ρ from 75 to 929 while the ρ bias held at −0.107 and −0.101 and the β₁ bias at +0.197 and +0.194. Lengthen the chain to cut the Monte Carlo error in one chain's summaries — at the defaults the effective sample size for ρ is only about 100, so vcov and the posterior mean carry a few percent of noise — but read a small-N ρ as biased toward zero whatever the chain length. Check accept_rate sits in 0.2–0.5. When N is small and ρ is the quantity of interest, fit_spatial_probit is the better choice: its ρ bias runs the other way and is smaller from N = 64 up (+0.044 at 64, +0.008 at 144, against −0.112 and −0.058 here on the same data).

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialDiscreteChoice.fit_spatial_ordered_probit — Function
fit_spatial_ordered_probit(W, X, y; n_categories=maximum(y), n_samples=2000, ...)

Fit the spatial ordered probit

y* = ρWy* + Xβ + ε,    y = j  iff  τ_{j-1} < y* ≤ τ_j

by Gibbs sampling with data augmentation (Wang, Kockelman & Wang, 2013). y holds integer categories 1:J.

For identification there is no intercept and τ_1 = 0; the free cutpoints τ_2 … τ_{J-1} are drawn from their uniform conditionals between the adjacent order statistics of the latent vector. τ_0 = -∞ and τ_J = +∞.

A binary y (J = 2) reduces to the probit above and is better served by fit_bayesian_spatial_probit, which carries an intercept.

The weak identification described for the binary sampler applies here too, and the free cutpoints move with it. Over 80 replications at N = 64, J = 3, ρ = 0.35: ρ bias −0.031, β biases +0.096 and −0.068, coverage 93.8%, 95.0% and 91.2%. τ_2 came out +0.103 high, the same proportional inflation as ‖β‖, because cutpoints and coefficients share the latent scale. τ_1 is exactly 0 in every draw by construction.

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialDiscreteChoice.fit_spatial_probit — Function
fit_spatial_probit(W, X, y; varnames=nothing, instrument_lags=2)

Fit the spatial autoregressive probit model

y* = ρWy* + Xβ + ε,    y = 1(y* > 0)

by the linearized GMM estimator of Klier & McMillen (2008). A standard probit supplies the ρ = 0 starting value; the moment conditions are then linearized around it and estimated by 2SLS with instruments [X, WX, W²X].

y must be coded 0/1. Returns a SpatialProbitModel.

Fitted probabilities use the heteroskedastic latent form Φ(((I-ρW)⁻¹Xβ)ᵢ / √[(I-ρW)⁻¹(I-ρW)⁻ᵀ]ᵢᵢ), which needs a dense inverse of I - ρW; cost is O(N³), so this is intended for moderate N.

loglikelihood reports the probit log-likelihood evaluated at the fitted index. It is a pseudo-likelihood — the estimator is GMM, not ML — so it is comparable across specifications fit the same way, but is not an ML criterion.

Linearizing around ρ = 0 buys speed at a real cost in efficiency. Over 8 replications at ρ = 0.4 this estimator had a standard deviation of about 0.25 at N = 196 and 0.18 at N = 400 — roughly 2.5× that of fit_bayesian_spatial_probit on the same data. It is approximately unbiased, so treat a single small-sample ρ̂ as a coarse reading and prefer the Gibbs sampler when precision on ρ matters.

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialDiscreteChoice.marginal_effects — Function
marginal_effects(m::SpatialProbitModel)

Decompose the marginal effect of each covariate into direct, indirect, and total components. For covariate k the effect matrix is diag(φ(index)) (I-ρW)⁻¹ βₖ; the direct effect averages its diagonal, the total averages its row sums, and the indirect effect is the difference (LeSage & Pace, 2009).

Requires a dense inverse of I - ρW — O(N³), so intended for moderate N.
