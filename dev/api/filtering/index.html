Spatial Filtering

Eigenvector spatial filtering (ESF), its random-effects variant, and spatio-temporal principal components.

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialFiltering.ESFModel — Type
ESFModel

An eigenvector spatial filtering fit, returned by fit_esf.

Spatial structure is absorbed by adding selected eigenvectors of the doubly-centred weight matrix to the design as synthetic regressors, chosen stepwise. Because they are orthogonal to the intercept and to each other, the covariate coefficients stay interpretable as ordinary regression coefficients — the appeal of the method over a lag model.

Inspect the chosen basis with selected_eigenvectors.

Note

Selecting zero eigenvectors is a valid outcome, not a failure: it means the residuals were already free of spatial autocorrelation.

source
SpatialEconometrics.SpatialFiltering.REESFModel — Type
REESFModel

A random-effects eigenvector spatial filtering fit, returned by fit_re_esf.

Instead of selecting eigenvectors stepwise as fit_esf does, this keeps the whole screened pool and shrinks every coefficient towards zero, governed by one variance ratio theta = tau2/sigma2 estimated by profile REML. That avoids the model-selection variance stepwise search introduces.

Warning

The reported residual Moran coefficient is for the conditional residual and is supposed to be substantially negative — do not compare it against zero. Subtracting the fitted spatial term leaves the remainder in the orthogonal complement of the retained eigenvectors, which is itself negatively autocorrelated. Compare it against moran_reference, the closed-form expectation stored on the model.

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialFiltering.STPCAResult — Type
STPCAResult

Spatio-temporal principal components, as returned by stpca.

Fields

  • values: eigenvalues of E, descending. Each is the value of the maximised objective, Var(U_k) · A(Aγ_k), for its own component.
  • vectors: the directions γ_k, one per column, orthonormal.
  • scores: n × K, the units' positions on each component, A γ_k.
  • variance: Var(U_k) = γ_k' S γ_k, the first factor of the objective.
  • autocorr: the second factor — Geary's C or Moran's I of that component's scores.
  • proportion: λ_k / Σ|λ|. For :geary every eigenvalue is non-negative, so this is the ordinary share; for :moran it is signed.
  • coefs: n × B, the column-centred basis coefficients A.
  • measure: :geary or :moran.
  • n_basis, n_features, interval, varnames: what the expansion used.

See also stpca, feature_contributions, component_weight.

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialFiltering.SpatialEigen — Type
SpatialEigen

A screened eigenvector basis for spatial filtering, as returned by spatial_eigenvectors.

Fields

  • values::Vector{T}: eigenvalues of Ω = M Wᵤ M, in descending order.
  • vectors::Matrix{T}: the corresponding eigenvectors, one per column. Columns are orthonormal and each sums to zero.
  • moran::Vector{T}: each eigenvector's Moran coefficient, (N/S₀)λᵢ.
  • S0::T: the sum of all weights, Σᵢⱼ wᵢⱼ.
  • N::Int: the number of spatial units.

Pass an instance as the eigen_basis keyword of fit_esf or fit_re_esf to reuse one decomposition across several fits.

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialFiltering.component_groups — Function
component_groups(m)

The positive and negative components of m, as index vectors into its spectrum, in the order the reference reports them.

Under measure = :moran the sign of an eigenvalue is a substantive result, not a numerical detail: a positive one is a global component, where linked units carry similar scores, and a negative one is a local component, where a unit's score is surrounded by unlike ones. The reference orders the positive group by decreasing eigenvalue and the negative group by decreasing absolute eigenvalue — the strongest local structure first — and this returns (positive = …, negative = …) in that order. m.values itself is sorted descending throughout, so the negative group appears there in the opposite order.

Under measure = :geary the construction is non-negative definite, so the negative group is empty up to rounding and the whole spectrum is the positive one.

Zero eigenvalues are in neither group

B = p · n_basis directions exist, but at most min(n - 1, B) carry variance. The rest have eigenvalues at rounding level and are excluded from both groups by tol.

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialFiltering.component_weight — Function
component_weight(m, k, t)

The vector weighting function u_k(t) of component k, evaluated at t: a length(t) × p matrix whose column j is the weight the component puts on feature j at each moment.

A spatio-temporal component is a direction in coefficient space, and this is what that direction looks like as a function of time — the functional analogue of a loading. Feature j is the one the component is reading at moment t when |u_kj(t)| is the largest of the p entries.

See also feature_contributions.

source
SpatialEconometrics.SpatialFiltering.feature_contributions — Function
feature_contributions(m, k; n_grid = 512)

How much each feature contributes to component k, as the share of the area under |u_kj(t)| over the expansion interval:

\[A^*_j = A_j \Big/ \sum_{i=1}^p A_i, \qquad A_j = \int_I |u_{kj}(t)|\,dt\]

Returns a Vector of p shares summing to one, in the order of m.varnames. The feature with the largest share is the one the component is mostly made of, which is the reading the reference reports alongside the component scores — a component is only interpretable once you know which of the p series it is tracking.

The integral is taken by the trapezoid rule on n_grid points, since |u_kj| has corners wherever u_kj crosses zero and no closed form.

See also component_weight.

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialFiltering.fit_esf — Function
fit_esf(X, y, W; family=:gaussian, max_eigenvectors=20, criterion=:moran, ...)

Eigenvector spatial filtering (Griffith, 2003). Selected eigenvectors of the doubly-centred W enter as extra regressors, absorbing the spatial autocorrelation so the remaining coefficients can be read as aspatial:

\[g(\mu) = \alpha + X\beta + E_{sel}\gamma\]

family is :gaussian (OLS), :poisson, :logit, or :negbin; the last three are fit by IRLS. The negative binomial's dispersion is re-estimated between IRLS passes, by its own maximum likelihood (dispersion=:mle, the default) or by the method of moments of Chun et al. (2016) (dispersion=:moments). This makes ESF usable wherever a GLM is, which parametric SAR/SEM cannot match.

Eigenvectors are added greedily. criterion=:moran picks, at each step, the candidate that most reduces the absolute Moran coefficient of the residuals (Pearson residuals for GLM families); criterion=:aic picks the one that most reduces AIC. Selection stops at max_eigenvectors, when no candidate improves the criterion, or — under :moran — once |MC| falls below moran_tol.

min_moran_ratio screens the candidate pool to eigenvectors with MCᵢ/max(MC) ≥ ratio (default 0.25, the usual positive-SA screen). Pass a precomputed SpatialEigen as eigen_basis to reuse one decomposition across several fits.

Cost is O(max_eigenvectors × n_candidates) model fits, so keep the pool bounded for GLM families.

Inference after selection

The standard errors treat the selected basis as if it had been fixed in advance. It was not: the eigenvectors were chosen on the same y, by a greedy search over the whole candidate pool. Coverage of a nominal 95% interval for the covariate slopes is therefore below 95% whenever eigenvectors are actually selected. Over 400 replications at N = 64 with SAR(0.6) errors, about 5 eigenvectors selected per fit:

β₁β₂
nominal95%95%
measured91.5%90.5%

With no spatial autocorrelation almost nothing is selected and coverage is right (94.8%, 94.5%). So read the slopes as point estimates whose intervals are mildly optimistic, and treat the gap as the cost of letting the data pick the basis. fit_re_esf avoids the problem by shrinking a fixed basis instead of selecting from it, and its intervals are not subject to this caveat.

The GLM families

All four log-likelihoods match the corresponding Distributions.logpdf to 1e-10, and IRLS reaches the actual maximum: at the returned β̂ the score of an independently written log-likelihood vanishes to 1e-13 and a Newton step moves by 1e-16.

vcov is the inverse of the IRLS information X′WX. The log and logit links are canonical for the Poisson and the Bernoulli, so that is also the observed Hessian; the coverage those two reach is in the table below.

The log link is not canonical for NB2, so X′WX is the expected information there and genuinely differs from the Hessian. That is the right choice — the NB2 information matrix is block diagonal in β and α, so estimating the dispersion does not inflate Var(β̂).

The dispersion itself is estimated by maximum likelihood: the root of the profile score for α at the current μ, found by Newton on log(1/α) within a maintained bracket. The alternative, dispersion=:moments, is the Pearson moment estimate of Chun et al. (2016), kept because that is the scheme the method was published with. The MLE is the more precise of the two at every sample size: at a true α = 0.5 over 3000 samples its root mean squared error is 0.199 against the moment estimate's 0.285 at N = 81, and 0.046 against 0.067 at N = 1500 — about a third off throughout — and it is the closer of the two on roughly two samples in three. Its median bias at N = 81 is −8.7% against −16.2%.

The moment estimate also reports a dispersion on the positivity floor far more often: on the N = 81 coverage design below it does so for 5.6% of fits against the MLE's 0.7%. Those fits keep the working weights at their Poisson values, and their slope intervals cover 13 to 19 points worse than the same scheme's other fits.

Coverage of a nominal 95% slope interval at N = 81, over 6000 replications on the design described above:

So the dispersion is no longer what holds the negative binomial back: on :mle it covers as well as the Poisson does. What the three count and binary families still have in common is the post-selection effect described above under Inference after selection — the mean standard error is 0.92 of β̂'s actual sampling standard deviation for every one of them, :moments and :mle alike — and that, not the dispersion, is what the last two points are.

Identification

The design has to have full column rank. X goes in without an intercept, since one is added, and no covariate may be a linear combination of the others. A design that fails this throws an ArgumentError naming the offending column, rather than failing inside the linear algebra or returning an arbitrary split of one coefficient across two.

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialFiltering.fit_re_esf — Function
fit_re_esf(X, y, W; min_moran_ratio=0.25, n_candidates=nothing, varnames=nothing)

Random-effects eigenvector spatial filtering (Murakami & Griffith, 2015):

\[y = X\beta + E\gamma + \varepsilon, \quad \gamma \sim N(0, \tau^2\Lambda), \quad \varepsilon \sim N(0, \sigma^2 I)\]

Rather than selecting eigenvectors discretely, every positive-eigenvalue eigenvector is retained and its coefficient shrunk toward zero, with the shrinkage governed by the single variance ratio θ = τ²/σ² estimated by profile REML. This sidesteps the model-selection variance of stepwise ESF.

Because E has orthonormal columns, V = σ²(I + θEΛE') inverts in closed form — its eigenvalues are 1 + θλₗ inside span(E) and 1 outside — so each REML evaluation is O(NL), not O(N³). The decomposition itself is still O(N³).

Only positive-eigenvalue eigenvectors are used, the standard choice for positive spatial autocorrelation, and min_moran_ratio screens them further to MCᵢ/max(MC) ≥ ratio.

Reading the residual Moran coefficient

moran_final is the Moran coefficient of the conditional residual y - Xβ̂ - Eγ̂, and it is expected to be substantially negative — do not compare it against zero. Subtracting Eγ̂ removes the positively autocorrelated directions, leaving a residual that lives mostly in the orthogonal complement of span(E); a vector drawn at random from that complement has Moran coefficient

\[\frac{N}{S_0}\cdot\frac{-S_0/N - \sum_l \lambda_l}{N - 1 - L}\]

which is reported as moran_reference. On correctly specified data with N = 121 and L = 40 this reference is −0.304 and the fitted residual lands near −0.24, i.e. slightly less negative than chance. A moran_final close to or above moran_reference is the healthy case; one far below it suggests the random effects are absorbing noise, as does an edf (effective degrees of freedom, P + Σ θλ/(1+θλ)) approaching N.

Identification

The design has to have full column rank. X goes in without an intercept, since one is added, and no covariate may be a linear combination of the others. A design that fails this throws an ArgumentError naming the offending column, rather than failing inside the linear algebra or returning an arbitrary split of one coefficient across two.

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialFiltering.fourier_basis — Function
fourier_basis(times, n_basis; interval = nothing)

Evaluate the first n_basis orthonormal Fourier basis functions at times, returning a length(times) × n_basis matrix.

The basis on an interval of length L is 1/√L, then √(2/L)·sin(2πkt/L) and √(2/L)·cos(2πkt/L) for k = 1, 2, …, taken in that order and truncated at n_basis. It is orthonormal with respect to ∫ over the interval, which is what lets the functional inner product ⟨u, X⟩ be computed as the plain dot product of coefficient vectors.

The reference's construction is one constant plus whole sin/cos pairs, so an odd n_basis is the natural choice — 5 is the reference's own, and the default here. An even n_basis is allowed and still orthonormal, but it ends on a sine with its cosine missing, which is a basis the reference does not use.

interval defaults to times padded by half the mean spacing at each end. The padding is not cosmetic: a Fourier basis is periodic, so an interval running exactly from the first observation to the last forces the fitted curve to take the same value at both, which is a constraint the data have no reason to satisfy. Krzyśko et al. observe 2002–2018 at t = 0.5, 1.5, …, 16.5 and expand on [0, 17], which is this default.

See also stpca.

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialFiltering.spatial_eigenvectors — Function
spatial_eigenvectors(W; n_candidates=nothing, min_moran_ratio=0.0)

Eigendecomposition of the doubly-centred weight matrix Ω = (I - 11'/N) Wsym (I - 11'/N), following Griffith (2003).

The eigenvectors are mutually orthogonal and uncorrelated, and each carries Moran coefficient MCᵢ = (N/S₀)λᵢ. Eigenvectors with large positive eigenvalues describe positive spatial autocorrelation patterns; large negative eigenvalues describe negative ones. Results are sorted by eigenvalue, descending.

An asymmetric W (e.g. row-standardised) is symmetrised as (W + W')/2 so the eigenvalues are real.

The constant vector is removed by construction rather than by discarding an eigenvector afterwards: with Q an orthonormal basis of 1's orthogonal complement, Q'ΩQ = Q'WsymQ, so eigendecomposing the reduced (N-1)×(N-1) matrix and mapping back as E = QV yields vectors that are orthonormal and exactly mean-zero. Decomposing Ω directly would not: its null space is multi-dimensional, the solver may return any rotation within it, and the constant direction then smears across several near-zero eigenvectors that are each partly collinear with the intercept.

n_candidates keeps only the leading eigenvectors; min_moran_ratio keeps those with MCᵢ / max(MC) ≥ ratio, the usual screen for positive-SA filtering (0.25 is a common choice). Both are applied after sorting.

Dense eigen is O(N³) — intended for moderate N.

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialFiltering.stpca — Function
stpca(X, W; measure = :geary, n_basis = 5, times = axes(X, 3), kwargs...)

Spatio-temporal principal components: directions that maximise the product of a functional principal component's variance and the spatial autocorrelation of the scores it produces.

X is n × p × T — n spatial units, p features, T observation times — or n × T for a single feature. Each unit-feature time series is first expanded in n_basis orthonormal Fourier functions by least squares, giving an n × B coefficient matrix A with B = p · n_basis; the components are then found in that coefficient space, so time enters through the expansion and space through W.

With S = A'A/n, a functional principal component U = γ'α has estimated variance γ'Sγ, and its scores across units are y = Aγ. Writing the spatial measure of those scores as a ratio of quadratic forms, the product of the two factors collapses to a single eigenproblem:

\[\hat{\mathrm{Var}}(U)\,\mathcal{A}(A\gamma) = \gamma' E \gamma, \qquad \|\gamma\| = 1\]

so the components are the orthonormal eigenvectors of E, and each eigenvalue is the product being maximised. For measure = :geary, E = ((n-1)/(2na))·A'B*A where a = Σᵢⱼwᵢⱼ and B* is the symmetrised contiguity matrix of Geary's ratio; for measure = :moran, E = (n/(a·n))·A'·((W + W')/2)·A.

Keywords

  • measure: :geary (default, Krzyśko et al. 2023) or :moran.
  • n_basis: Fourier functions per feature. Must not exceed T.
  • times: observation times, 1:T by default.
  • interval: expansion interval, padded by half the mean spacing by default — see fourier_basis.
  • varnames: feature names, used in feature_contributions.
The two measures do not order the spectrum the same way

Moran's I is large when linked units agree, so measure = :moran puts smooth, global structure at the top and leaves the negative eigenvalues — the local components — at the bottom. That split is sharp: over 800 planted draws a smooth field landed on the first component in 73–87% of them and a high-contrast field on the last in 72–86%, and neither ever landed at the wrong end.

Geary's C runs the other way, large when linked units differ, so maximising Var · C demotes smooth structure — in those same draws a smooth planted field was never the first :geary component. But the :geary ordering is not the reverse of the :moran one: the high-contrast field led only 7–34% of the time, because the objective is a product and a moderate-C component with large variance outranks a high-C component with little. Read the :geary ordering as "variance weighted by dissimilarity", not as "most dissimilar first".

The whole spectrum is returned either way, so both ends are always available. What is not safe is reading "the first component" without saying which measure produced it. docs/AUDIT.md has the rates.

Why `E` is non-negative definite under `:geary`

γ'Eγ is a variance times a Geary ratio, and both factors are non-negative, so every eigenvalue is too — rank(E) of them positive and the rest zero. Under :moran the second factor can be negative, which is exactly the global/local split the eigenvalue signs carry.

Examples

m = stpca(X, W; n_basis = 5, times = 0.5:1:16.5, interval = (0.0, 17.0))
m.scores[:, 1]                 # where the units sit on the first component
feature_contributions(m, 1)    # which feature drives it

References

measure = :moran is equation (21) of Krzyśko, M., Nijkamp, P., Ratajczak, W., Wołyński, W. & Wenerska, B. (2024). Spatio-temporal principal component analysis. Spatial Economic Analysis, 19(1), 8–29, with the symmetrisation of its equation (23).

measure = :geary is equation (4) of Krzyśko, M., Nijkamp, P., Ratajczak, W., Wołyński, W., Wojtyła, A. & Wenerska, B. (2023). A novel spatio-temporal principal component analysis based on Geary's contiguity ratio. Computers, Environment and Urban Systems, 103, 101980.

Both rest on the spatial PCA of Jombart, T., Devillard, S., Dufour, A.-B. & Pontier, D. (2008). Revealing cryptic spatial patterns in genetic variability by a new multivariate method. Heredity, 101, 92–103, and on the multivariate functional PCA of Górecki et al. (2018).
