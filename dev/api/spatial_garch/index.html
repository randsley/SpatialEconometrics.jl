Spatial log-GARCH

API reference for SpatialGARCH. See Spatial log-GARCH for the discussion, including what identifies λ and what a covariate-distance W costs.

Module

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialGARCH — Module
SpatialGARCH

Spatial log-GARCH for a cross-section (Sato & Matsuda 2021): spatial dependence in the conditional variance rather than in the mean.

y_i = √h_i ε_i,    log h = λW log h + ρW log y² + α1 + Zδ,

with ε_i i.i.d., E ε_i² = 1, and one row-standardised W. Writing Y = log y², U = log ε², c = E U, V = U − c and R(x) = I − xW, the model is a SARMA(1,1) in Y:

R(λ + ρ) Y = Xβ + R(λ) V,    X = [1, Z],  β = (α + (1 − λ)c, δ).

Sato & Matsuda estimate it in two steps: Gaussian QML of the SARMA for ψ = (β, σ², λ, ρ) (their Theorems 3.1–3.2: consistent and asymptotically normal under a sandwich covariance), then α in closed form from the E ε² = 1 normalisation (Theorem 3.3: consistent, no distribution theory).

The score covariance Ω behind the sandwich is not given explicitly in the paper or its supplement. It is derived here: at the true ψ every score component is linear-quadratic in the i.i.d. V, so Ω has an exact closed form in σ² and the third and fourth moments of V (SpatialUtils._lq_cov). The test suite checks it against simulation.

The covariate-distance W that Fülle & Otto (2024) pair with this model is covariate_weights, in SpatialWeights.

Estimation

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialGARCH.SpatialLogGARCHModel — Type
SpatialLogGARCHModel

A fitted spatial log-GARCH, returned by fit_spatial_log_garch.

Fields:

  • coef, vcov, coefnames: (δ, λ, ρ) and their sandwich covariance Ĥ⁻¹Ω̂Ĥ⁻¹, which allows any distribution of ε with E ε² = 1 and finite fourth moment of log ε².
  • vcov_gaussian: −Ĥ⁻¹ for the same parameters. It is valid only if log ε² were normal. It never is when ε is normal: log χ²₁ has excess kurtosis 4. It is kept for comparison, not for inference.
  • alpha: the volatility intercept from step 2. It has no standard error: Sato & Matsuda prove it consistent but give no distribution.
  • beta0: the intercept of the log y² equation, α + (1 − λ)c, a nuisance that mixes α with c = E log ε². Its variance is in vcov_full.
  • vcov_full, fullnames: the sandwich for all of (β, σ², λ, ρ).
  • sigma2, mu3, mu4: the variance and the third and fourth central moments of V = log ε² − c, from the step-1 residuals.
  • log_volatility: the fitted log ĥ.
  • loglik: the Gaussian quasi log-likelihood of the SARMA at the estimate.
  • iterations, gradient_norm: the Newton iterations, and the final max|∂ℓ/∂θ| / n for θ = (λ, λ + ρ).

SpatialEconometrics.SpatialGARCH.fit_spatial_log_garch — Function
fit_spatial_log_garch(y, W; Z = nothing, varnames = nothing,
                      eigenvalues = nothing, bounds = (-0.99, 0.99),
                      tol = 1e-8, maxiter = 100) -> SpatialLogGARCHModel

Fit the spatial log-GARCH of Sato & Matsuda (2021) to a cross-section y:

y_i = √h_i ε_i,    log h = λW log h + ρW log y² + α1 + Zδ.

y is mean-zero data: returns, or the residuals of a mean model (Sato & Matsuda fit SAR residuals). W must be row-standardised (every row sums to 1); the theory covers one W in both places. Z holds optional exogenous variables, without an intercept.

Step 1 maximises the Gaussian quasi log-likelihood of the SARMA(1,1) form R(λ+ρ) log y² = Xβ + R(λ)V over θ = (λ, λ + ρ), with β and σ² profiled out: a 21 × 21 grid inside bounds, then Newton steps with step halving until max|∂ℓ/∂θ|/n < tol. The fit throws if that is not reached; it never returns a point that does not solve the first-order conditions. log|R(x)| and its traces come from the eigenvalues of W, computed once (O(n³)) or passed in with eigenvalues.

Step 2 sets α̂ = (1 − λ̂) log(n⁻¹ Σ exp C_i), with C = R(λ̂)⁻¹(R(λ̂ + ρ̂) log y² − Zδ̂) (eq. 11). It has no standard error.

Standard errors are the sandwich Ĥ⁻¹Ω̂Ĥ⁻¹. Ĥ is the analytic Hessian and Ω̂ the exact score covariance at the estimate, with the variance, third and fourth moments of log ε² taken from the residuals. The paper does not state Ω explicitly; it is derived here and checked by simulation in the test suite.

No interior maximum near λ = 1

Because W1 = 1, the intercept absorbs the part of the residual that R(λ)⁻¹ inflates as λ → 1, so σ̂²(λ) stays bounded while −log|R(λ)| grows like −log(1 − λ). The quasi log-likelihood is therefore unbounded above as λ → 1, in every sample (for a connected W). The estimate is the interior local maximum. When a sample has none, the search runs to the edge of bounds and the fit throws. In the package's Monte Carlo this happens in 36–39% of samples with (λ, ρ) = (0.9, 0.05) and n = 100 (rook W), and in none at n = 400.

Identification of λ

The mean of log y² depends on λ and ρ only through λ + ρ. λ is identified separately only by the moving-average part of the error, R(λ)V, so it is estimated less precisely than λ + ρ, and with an intercept alone (no Z) it rests on that part entirely. Sato & Matsuda assume identification (their Assumption 3.8) without stating this. The package's Monte Carlo measures what it costs; see the manual page.

Zeros

log y² is undefined at y_i = 0, so the fit throws if any y_i is zero. Rounded returns often have exact zeros; the model does not cover them.

Example

W = make_rook_weights(20)                       # 400 units
m = fit_spatial_log_garch(y, W; Z = z)
m.lambda, m.rho, m.alpha, stderror(m)
