Semiparametric Spatial Models

API reference for SemiparametricSpatial. See Semiparametric Models for the discussion, including what the identification diagnostic can and cannot claim.

Module

SpatialEconometrics.SemiparametricSpatial — Module
SemiparametricSpatial

Spatial autoregressions with an unknown smooth component — roadmap #13.

\[y = ρWy + Xβ + m(z) + ε\]

m is left unspecified and estimated by a local-polynomial smoother on z. Three things live here, and they share one first stage:

  1. fit_partially_linear_sar: Su & Jin's (2010) profile quasi-maximum likelihood. The smoother matrix S depends on z and the bandwidth only, not on ρ, so after residualising on (I − S) the concentrated likelihood has exactly the shape fit_spatial maximises. It keeps the log-determinant.
  2. closed_form_rho: Smirnov's (2022) closed-form ρ, computed from the same residualised y, Wy and W²y. No log-determinant.
  3. spectral_overlap: Geniaux's (2026) diagnostic of how much of W's dominant spatial modes the smoother can reproduce. A smoother that reproduces them competes with ρWy for the same variation — the "geoadditive trap" — and ρ loses its identification.

What the overlap check can and cannot say

A small overlap says only that the smoother does not compete with ρWy for W's dominant modes, given that W is the true interaction structure. That is statistical identification of ρ. It is not causal identification of a spillover: the endogenous and the correlated effects stay confounded (Manski's reflection problem), and in a cross-section spatial heterogeneity and spillovers are observationally equivalent, so a large overlap cannot say which allocation of the variation is true either (Gibbons & Overman 2012; Debarsy & Le Gallo 2024). Nothing in this module makes ρ̂, or the impacts computed from it, causal.

References

  • Su, L., & Jin, S. (2010). Profile quasi-maximum likelihood estimation of partially linear spatial autoregressive models. Journal of Econometrics, 157(1), 18–33.
  • Smirnov, O. A. (2022). Closed-form estimator for spatial autoregressive models. Geographical Analysis, 54(1), 3–31.
  • Geniaux, G. (2026). Flexible nonlinear spatial autoregressive models under spatial confounding. arXiv:2611.16592 (preprint).
  • Lee, L.-F. (2004). Asymptotic distributions of quasi-maximum likelihood estimators for spatial autoregressive models. Econometrica, 72(6), 1899–1925.
  • Gibbons, S., & Overman, H. G. (2012). Mostly pointless spatial econometrics? Journal of Regional Science, 52(2), 172–191.
  • Debarsy, N., & Le Gallo, J. (2024). Identification of spatial spillovers: Do's and dont's. LEM working paper DT2024-03.

The smoother and the identification diagnostic

SpatialEconometrics.SemiparametricSpatial.local_polynomial_smoother — Function
local_polynomial_smoother(z, h; order = 1)

The n×n smoother matrix S of an order-r local-polynomial regression on z, so that the fitted values of any v regressed on z are S * v.

Row i is e₁'(D'KD)⁻¹D'K, with D the local design [1, (z_j − z_i)/h, …] up to total degree order and K the standardised Epanechnikov kernel weights, as in Su & Jin (2010, eq. 2.6 and §6).

z is an n×q matrix (a vector is one column); each column is divided by its standard deviation first, so h is in standard-deviation units. The kernel has compact support of radius √5 h, which their Assumption 5 requires.

order = 1 (local linear) is enough for q ≤ 3; their Assumption 5 needs order > q/2 − 1, so q ≥ 4 needs order = 2, and larger q is refused. Only orders 1 and 2 are offered: order 0 (Nadaraya–Watson) has the boundary bias Su & Jin exclude it for.

Local-polynomial smoothers reproduce polynomials of degree ≤ order exactly, so S * ones(n) == ones(n) to rounding. That is why an intercept must not be in X for fit_partially_linear_sar: m absorbs it.

Windows that hold too few points are widened. A compact kernel at a fixed h can leave an observation in the tail of z with too few points in its window to fit the local polynomial. That observation's window is then widened just past its 2p + 1-th nearest neighbour, p being the number of local-polynomial terms. Su & Jin's conditions have nh^q → ∞, so the share of widened windows vanishes as n grows; in finite samples it is reported as n_widened on fit_partially_linear_sar's result. Without this guard, 221 of 500 fits failed at n = 400 in the Monte Carlo of docs/AUDIT.md. An ArgumentError is thrown only if a local design is singular even after widening.

Dense: O(n²) memory, intended for n up to a few thousand.

SpatialEconometrics.SemiparametricSpatial.SpectralOverlapResult — Type
SpectralOverlapResult

The spectral projection diagnostic of Geniaux (2026, Appendix B.6).

Fields

  • pi::Vector — π_k = ‖S φ_k‖ for each of the k dominant eigenvectors φ_k: the share of that spatial mode the smoother can reproduce, between 0 (none) and about 1 (all of it).
  • pibar — the mean of pi, Geniaux's summary π̄.
  • eigenvalues::Vector — the eigenvalues of the doubly-centred symmetrised W belonging to those modes, descending.
  • moran::Vector — the Moran coefficient of each mode.

This is a measure, not a test

The source gives no threshold and no null distribution for π̄; its Lucas County application reports 0.06 under 4-nearest-neighbour weights and 0.26 under 10-nearest-neighbour weights, and reads the second as a configuration where identification needs scrutiny. There is accordingly no p-value here and no verdict, and none should be attached.

A small π̄ says the smoother does not compete with ρWy for W's dominant modes, given that W is the true interaction structure. It does not make ρ̂ causal; see the module docstring.

SpatialEconometrics.SemiparametricSpatial.spectral_overlap — Function
spectral_overlap(S, W; k = 25)

How much of each of W's k dominant spatial modes the smoother S can reproduce: π_j = ‖S φ_j‖ for the leading eigenvectors φ_j (Geniaux 2026, Appendix B.6 and Figure B.10, which uses k = 25).

The modes are the eigenvectors of the doubly-centred symmetrised W, (I − 11'/n)(W + W')/2 (I − 11'/n), from spatial_eigenvectors. Geniaux states the eigenvectors of the symmetrised W itself. The centring is a deliberate departure: every smoother here reproduces constants, and the top eigenvector of an uncentred row-standardised W is nearly constant, so its overlap would read close to 1 for any fit and say nothing. The constant is absorbed by m in any case and is not a mode ρ competes for.

Returns a SpectralOverlapResult. Dense eigendecomposition: O(n³).

Partially linear SAR (Su & Jin 2010)

SpatialEconometrics.SemiparametricSpatial.fit_partially_linear_sar — Function
fit_partially_linear_sar(y, X, z, W; bandwidth = :rule_of_thumb, order = 1,
                         method = ChebyshevApprox(order = 6),
                         rho_bounds = (-0.995, 0.995), overlap_k = 25,
                         varnames = nothing, max_exact_n = 1000,
                         vcov_probes = 250, rng = Random.default_rng())

Su & Jin's (2010) profile quasi-maximum likelihood estimator of

\[y = ρWy + Xβ + m(z) + ε,\]

with m unknown and ε i.i.d.

For fixed ρ, m is estimated by a local-polynomial regression of (I − ρW)y − Xβ on z (local_polynomial_smoother). The smoother S does not depend on ρ, so β(ρ) is least squares of (I − S)(I − ρW)y on (I − S)X, σ²(ρ) is a quadratic in ρ, and ρ̂ maximises

\[\ell(ρ) = -\tfrac{n}{2}\log σ^2(ρ) + \log|I - ρW| + \text{const}.\]

The log-determinant is kept: method chooses ChebyshevApprox (the default, as in fit_spatial) or ExactLU. Then m̂ = S((I − ρ̂W)y − Xβ̂).

Arguments

  • X must not contain an intercept or any function of z alone: the smoother reproduces constants and absorbs both, and the design check then refuses the fit as rank deficient. Pass zeros(n, 0) for no X.
  • z: n×q (or a vector), standardised internally.
  • bandwidth: a number (in standard deviations of z), :rule_of_thumb for Su & Jin's h = n^{−1/(q+4)} (§6), or :lscv for their two-step recommendation: fit at the rule of thumb, then choose h by least-squares cross-validation of y − Xβ̃ − ρ̃Wy on z and refit.
  • overlap_k: modes for the spectral_overlap diagnostic, which is computed on every fit.

Variance

vcov is the inverse of Su & Jin's information matrix (their Theorem 4.3): Lee's (2004) SAR information with P = (I − S)'(I − S) weighting the X and R = G(Xβ + m) blocks, G = W(I − ρW)⁻¹. Their full sandwich adds an Ω built from the third and fourth moments of ε; Ω is zero under normality and is not included, so the standard errors assume normal errors. Their coverage under non-normal errors is measured, not assumed: see docs/AUDIT.md.

Assumptions that matter

Errors must be homoskedastic (their Assumption 2: under heteroskedasticity the QMLE is generally inconsistent). Rates are √n when each unit has a bounded number of neighbours. Identification (Assumption 7) needs GXβ, after (I − S) and the projection off X, to stay non-degenerate — exactly what a smoother in the coordinates threatens. Check m.overlap.

Returns a PartiallyLinearSARModel.

Reference

Su, L., & Jin, S. (2010). Profile quasi-maximum likelihood estimation of partially linear spatial autoregressive models. Journal of Econometrics, 157(1), 18–33.

Closed-form ρ (Smirnov 2022; Geniaux 2026)

SpatialEconometrics.SemiparametricSpatial.ClosedFormRhoResult — Type
ClosedFormRhoResult

The closed-form ρ of closed_form_rho.

Fields

  • rho, beta (least squares at ρ̂ on the residualised design), sigma2.
  • se_classical — Smirnov's √(σ̂²/(b − 2ρ̂a)). Not heteroskedasticity-robust, and conservative: Geniaux (2026, Proposition 2 and Appendix C.6) reports it over-states the sampling variance, increasingly as ρ grows.
  • se_hc0 — √(Σmᵢ²)/d with mᵢ = (e₀ − ρ̂e₁)ᵢ(e₁ − ρ̂e₂)ᵢ, d = b − 2ρ̂a (Geniaux §2.5). Ignores the correlation between neighbouring mᵢ and under-covers in his check (0.90–0.92).
  • se_shac — adds Σ_{i≠j} mᵢmⱼ over pairs within graph distance two in W; close to nominal in his check.
  • a, b, c — the quadratic aρ² − bρ + c = 0 that was solved.
  • smoothed::Bool — whether the first stage carried a smoother. When it did, see the warning on closed_form_rho.

SpatialEconometrics.SemiparametricSpatial.closed_form_rho — Function
closed_form_rho(m::PartiallyLinearSARModel)
closed_form_rho(y, X, W)

Smirnov's (2022) closed-form estimator of ρ, which needs no log-determinant.

With e₀, e₁, e₂ the residuals of y, Wy and W²y on the first stage, ρ̂ is the smaller root of the quadratic quasi-score

\[aρ^2 - bρ + c = 0,\quad a = e_1'e_2,\; b = e_0'e_2 + e_1'e_1,\; c = e_0'e_1,\]

ρ̂ = (b − √(b² − 4ac)) / 2a (Smirnov 2022, eqs 11–16). The quasi-score keeps the first term of the power-series expansion of the ML score and sets the rest to their expectation, zero — which uses tr W = 0, so a W with a non-zero diagonal is refused.

The first method uses the partially linear model's own first stage, eₖ = M(I − S)Wᵏy with M the projection off (I − S)X: the same residuals that give its σ̂²(ρ) = ‖e₀ − ρe₁‖²/n. The second is Smirnov's linear estimator, with the first stage least squares on X (which should then include the intercept).

With a smoother, this estimator is unproven

Smirnov gives no consistency proof. Geniaux (2026) proves consistency and asymptotic normality for a fixed spline basis, where the smoother collapses onto a projection (his Lemma 1). A kernel smoother with a shrinking bandwidth is the sieve regime of his Remark 6, where rate conditions return and nothing is proved. Its coverage is measured in docs/AUDIT.md; read that before relying on any of its standard errors.

Returns a ClosedFormRhoResult.

Time-varying coefficients (Liang, Gao & Gong 2022)

SpatialEconometrics.SemiparametricSpatial.TVCoefSARModel — Type
TVCoefSARModel

A fit of Y_t = ρWY_t + X_tβ(t/T) + α + e_t by fit_tv_coef_sar.

Fields

  • rho, rho_se, sigma2, loglik.
  • beta::Matrix — T×d: row s is β̂(s/T).
  • beta_se::Matrix — pointwise standard errors of β̂(s/T), conditional on h, ρ̂ and α̂: σ̂²ΨΨ' for the linear smoother Ψ. They exclude the smoothing bias, of order h²β''(τ) (LGG Theorem 3). Measured (validation/mc_tv_coef.jl): at the cross-validated h, a nominal 95% band covers β(τ) only 0.81–0.87 of the time in the interior, though 0.92–0.96 at the ends. The cause is the bias, plus the variation of the chosen h between samples, which a conditional SE cannot see. Refitting at half the CV bandwidth brought interior coverage to 0.92–0.94, but at T = 25 it failed on up to 9% of samples for a strongly curved β.
  • tau::Vector — the rescaled times s/T.
  • alpha::Vector — fixed effects, normalised to sum to zero (LGG §2.1).
  • bandwidth (in units of rescaled time), bandwidth_rule.
  • residuals, N, T, coefnames (the columns of X).

SpatialEconometrics.SemiparametricSpatial.fit_tv_coef_sar — Function
fit_tv_coef_sar(y, X, W, N, T; bandwidth = :cv, method = ChebyshevApprox(order = 6),
                rho_bounds = (-0.995, 0.995), varnames = nothing,
                max_exact_n = 1000, vcov_probes = 250, rng = Random.default_rng())

Liang, Gao & Gong's (2022) local-linear concentrated QML for the spatial panel with time-varying coefficients and individual fixed effects:

\[Y_t = ρWY_t + X_tβ(t/T) + α + e_t,\qquad t = 1,…,T.\]

ρ is a constant; only the regressor coefficients vary, as smooth functions of rescaled time τ = t/T. A time-varying ρ is not this model and is not offered: no published method for it exists (ROADMAP #13).

The data are stacked as elsewhere in the package's panels: N units within each of T periods, so y[(t−1)N + i] is unit i at time t. Put a column of ones in X for a time-varying intercept; the fixed effects are then normalised to sum to zero, as in the paper (§2.1).

Method (LGG §2.2)

  1. For fixed (ρ, α), β(τ) is the local-linear kernel regression of (I − ρW)Y_t − α on X_t around τ (Epanechnikov kernel, bandwidth h in units of rescaled time). The smoother S does not depend on (ρ, α).
  2. Partial α out of the (I − S)-residualised data, and maximise the concentrated log-likelihood, which keeps T log|I − ρW|.
  3. Update β̂(τ) at (ρ̂, α̂).

bandwidth is a number, or :cv for the paper's leave-one-unit-out cross-validation (Appendix A.3) on a 15-point log grid from 2/T to 1, with pilot (ρ̃, α̃) from a fit at h = T^{−1/5}. (The paper takes the pilot ρ̃ from the time-invariant Lee–Yu model; using the model's own fit keeps the pilot inside the model being tuned.)

Inference

rho_se is the inverse of LGG's Σ₀ under normal errors (their Proposition 1): R'PR/σ² + T(tr G² + tr G'G) against σ², with P = (I − S)'Q(I − S). The Ω term carrying the third and fourth error moments (Theorem 2) is omitted, so the standard error assumes normality. Rates: √(NT) for ρ, √(NTh) for β(τ), as both N and T grow (Assumption 3: Th → ∞, NTh⁸ → 0). The errors must be serially and cross-sectionally independent and homoskedastic (Assumption 2). See TVCoefSARModel for what beta_se does and does not cover.

Reference

Liang, X., Gao, J., & Gong, X. (2022). Semiparametric spatial autoregressive panel data model with fixed effects and time-varying coefficients. Journal of Business & Economic Statistics, 40(4), 1784–1802.
