Spatial Discrete Choice

Binary and ordered outcomes with spatial dependence — land use change, technology adoption, voting, firm entry/exit. Standard probit ignores the spatial correlation, giving inefficient estimates and invalid inference.

All three estimators target the spatial autoregressive latent model

\[y^* = \rho W y^* + X\beta + \varepsilon, \qquad \varepsilon \sim N(0, I)\]

with σ² = 1 fixed for identification.

Linearized GMM

Fast, no MCMC. A standard probit supplies the ρ = 0 starting value; the moment conditions are linearized around it and estimated by 2SLS with instruments [X, WX, W²X] (Klier & McMillen, 2008).

m = fit_spatial_probit(W, X, y; varnames=["gdp", "pop"])

y must be coded 0/1. Standard errors use a heteroskedasticity-robust 2SLS sandwich.

Efficiency cost

Linearizing around ρ = 0 trades precision for speed. Over 8 replications at ρ = 0.4 this estimator had a standard deviation of roughly 0.25 at N = 196 and 0.18 at N = 400 — about 2.5× that of the Gibbs sampler on the same data. It is approximately unbiased, so a single small-sample ρ̂ is a coarse reading. Prefer the Gibbs sampler when precision on ρ matters.

Bayesian (Albert–Chib)

Gibbs sampling with data augmentation, following LeSage & Pace (2009, Ch. 10). Each sweep draws the latent y* one site at a time from its truncated conditional, draws β from its conjugate normal conditional, then updates ρ by random-walk Metropolis.

m = fit_bayesian_spatial_probit(W, X, y; n_samples=2000, burnin=500)

Reported coef and vcov are the posterior mean and covariance. Raw draws are available as m.beta_draws and m.rho_draws.

Check the acceptance rate

m.accept_rate should land in roughly 0.2–0.5. The default rho_proposal_sd = 0.2 targets that band; 0.05 overshoots to about 0.77 and mixes slowly. Tune it if your acceptance rate falls outside the band.

Weak identification at small N

The observed signs pin the latent vector only up to scale, so ‖β‖ and ρ trade off along a weakly identified ridge. The posterior is centred correctly — the posterior mean averaged 0.374 across 8 replications at ρ = 0.4, N = 196 — but its spread is wide (SD ≈ 0.10). A single chain at small N can sit well away from the truth. Lengthen the chain and inspect m.rho_draws before reading much into one estimate.

Ordered probit

For y in categories 1:J, with τ_{j-1} < y* ≤ τ_j. Same Gibbs machinery, plus uniform conditional draws for the cutpoints (Wang, Kockelman & Wang, 2013).

m = fit_spatial_ordered_probit(W, X, y_ordered; n_categories=3)

For identification there is no intercept and τ_1 = 0; the free cutpoints τ_2 … τ_{J-1} are estimated. A binary y is better served by fit_bayesian_spatial_probit, which carries an intercept.

Marginal effects

Coefficients are not marginal effects in a spatial probit — a change in xᵢ propagates through (I - ρW)⁻¹. For covariate k the effect matrix is diag(φ(index)) (I-ρW)⁻¹ βₖ:

eff = marginal_effects(m)

The direct effect averages the diagonal, the total averages the row sums, and the indirect (spillover) effect is the difference (LeSage & Pace, 2009).

Cost

Fitted probabilities and marginal effects both need a dense inverse of I - ρW — O(N³). These estimators are intended for moderate N.
