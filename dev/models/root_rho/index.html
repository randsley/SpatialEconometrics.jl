Root Estimator of ρ

root_rho estimates ρ in

\[y = ρWy + Xβ + f + ε\]

where f is an unknown smooth nuisance, approximated in the span of a basis B that you supply: for example, a tensor spline or hat basis on the locations. Like closed_form_rho, it needs no log-determinant: ρ̂ is a root of a quadratic. Unlike it, it stays unbiased when the basis is large, and it reports an interval that stays valid when ρ is weakly identified.

r = root_rho(y, X, W; basis = B)
r.rho, r.se, r.ci_wald, r.cs

The basis can also be penalised, as a P-spline: see With a penalised smoother.

The moment

Write y₀(ρ) = y − ρWy and M = I − P_[X,B]. Smirnov's closed form solves (My₀)′W(My₀) = 0, a quadratic in ρ. At the true ρ its mean is tr(MWMΣ), which is not zero. It is of order K/n, where K is the number of columns of [X, B], so the estimator is consistent only when the basis is small against n. A basis that approximates a free surface well enough for valid inference is not small: at K = 191 and n = 900 the plain closed form is biased by about −0.5 (research/trace_cfe/).

Jin & Lee (2012) replace W by a matrix with a zero diagonal:

\[g(ρ) = y_0(ρ)'\,C\,y_0(ρ), \qquad C = (A − Δ)M,\quad Δ = \mathrm{Diag}(AM)\,\mathrm{Diag}(M)^{-1}.\]

In Jin & Lee's own notation (2012, p. 451), the first step solves

\[y_n'S_n'(ρ)\{W_n' − \mathrm{Diag}(W_n'M_{Z_n})[\mathrm{Diag}(M_{Z_n})]^{-1}\}M_{Z_n}S_n(ρ)y_n = 0,\]

and REsd uses $G_n'(\hat ρ_n) − \mathrm{Diag}[G_n'(\hat ρ_n)M_{Z_n}][\mathrm{Diag}(M_{Z_n})]^{-1}$ in place of the braces. Their S_n(ρ)y_n is y₀(ρ) here, M_{Z_n} is M, and the braces times M_{Z_n} are C.

Then diag C = 0 and C[X, B] = 0, so E g(ρ₀) = tr(CΣ) = 0 for any diagonal Σ: the moment is exact under heteroskedasticity of unknown form, whatever K is. root_rho takes two steps, as in Jin & Lee's REsd:

  1. A = W′ gives the first-step root ρ̃ (r.rho_first);
  2. A = G(ρ̃)′, with G(r) = W(I − rW)⁻¹, gives ρ̂ (r.rho).

The second step has a smaller variance. Each root is the one at which g′(ρ) < 0; the two roots of a quadratic have slopes of opposite sign, so that rule picks exactly one.

The rule assumes J = −E g′(ρ₀) > 0 (THEORY.md, A9). If J < 0, the other root is the consistent one, and the rule's root stays about |J/g₂| from ρ₀ however large n is. root_rho checks the sign at a 2SLS estimate (instruments [X, B, W[X, B]]) and warns if g′ is positive there, for the moment that gave ρ̂. J < 0 is rare: 0.7% of 3,000 hub-and-spoke designs had it, and in 1,000 fits on kNN designs the check never fired. In the hub design of testset 47g, the smoother's root is biased by 0.06 (about 2 SD) and its Wald interval covers 56 of 257 draws; CS, which does not use the rule, still contains ρ₀. REsd's second step, with A = G(ρ̃)′, had J > 0 even where the first step's was negative, so on the projection path the warning is rarer still.

Two intervals

The Wald interval is ρ̂ ± z·se, with se = √V̂(ρ̂)/|g′(ρ̂)|. V̂ is an HC2 variance (ûᵢ²/Mᵢᵢ) with a correction for the estimated P_[X,B]y₀ in the linear part of the moment.

The test-inversion set is

\[CS = \{ρ : g(ρ)^2 ≤ z^2\,\hat V(ρ)\},\]

an Anderson–Rubin-type set. It needs the moment's CLT at the true ρ only, not a non-vanishing slope, so it stays valid when the data say little about ρ. Then it is wide, and it may reach a bound of rho_bounds (r.cs_touches_bound) or split into more than one interval (r.cs_split). V̂(ρ) is an exact quartic in ρ, so the endpoints are exact roots, not grid points. Report CS. Where the two intervals agree, nothing is lost; where they differ, the Wald interval is the one that under-covers.

If the moment has no admissible root at either step, root_found is false, ρ̂ and the Wald interval are NaN, and CS is still returned.

When K/n is not small

The HC2 V̂ is consistent only when K/n → 0. When K/n → θ > 0, its relative bias at the true ρ, β_V = (E V̂ − V)/V, does not vanish. THEORY.md's Lemma 7 gives it exactly for any error law, and Proposition 6″ shows that CS then covers 2Φ(z√(1 + β_V)) − 1 for large n. Two conditions matter:

  • A local basis. V̂ concentrates around its mean (Lemma 10) when the projection on [X, B] draws on a bounded total weight of the units: ‖P_B‖_∞, its largest absolute row sum, is bounded. A tensor P-spline or hat basis has this property; polynomials of growing degree do not.

  • The error law. With homoskedastic symmetric errors, the bias is at least a worst case over error laws (Lemma 8). It is reached by ±σ (Rademacher) errors at f = 0 and computed from W and [X, B] alone. It is O(K/n) (Lemma 9). root_rho reports it for a projection as r.vhat_worst_bias, and show prints the coverage it implies:

      HC2 V̂: worst-case bias -2.1% (symmetric homoskedastic errors), so the set covers ≥ 94.8% for large n

    It was −0.4% to −2.7% in the designs measured (K/n = 0.06–0.40), a coverage of at least 94.7%. With heteroskedastic or skewed errors there is no design-only bound, and the bias was −1% to +20% where measured. An adversarial search over variance patterns does worse: with the largest variance up to 4 times the smallest, it found −4% to −12%, 3 to 6 times vhat_worst_bias, and −8% to −21% up to 10 times. Smooth variance patterns about halve that. At those patterns CS still covered 0.937–0.953. So vhat_worst_bias is the worst case only when all units share one error variance (research/tracecfe/tchc2_hetero.jl).

  • Skewed errors. With one error law for all units and a stated bound on its skewness, the worst case can still be computed from the design (Lemma 12). Skewness enters through the signal C′f, so the worst f is not 0. Pearson's bound (excess kurtosis ≥ skewness² − 2) fixes the worst kurtosis, and the worst f solves a 2 × 2 eigenvalue problem. Pass the bound as skewness:

    r = root_rho(y, X, W; basis = B, skewness = 2)
r.vhat_worst_bias       # the worst case over laws with |skewness| ≤ 2

    It changes only this diagnostic, not the estimate or the intervals. At skewness = 2 it was up to 2.7 times the symmetric worst case in the designs measured, and it grows with the number of neighbours in W. On a rook lattice it was no worse, because the kurtosis that skewness forces outweighs it. A different skewness for each unit can be 1.6 to 2 times worse again, and is not covered. It is NaN for the penalised smoother, where Lemma 8 does not apply.

Choosing the basis

The intervals are valid only when the approximation error of f is small against the sampling noise. That needs a basis larger than the one that fits f best:

  • Do not choose K by GCV or by cross-validated fit of f. In the research Monte Carlo those choices over-smoothed for inference.
  • Report ρ̂ and CS across a few basis sizes, as examples/34_root_rho.jl does. A ρ̂ that drifts as K grows is a sign that the smaller bases leave part of f in the residual.
  • The coverage was measured for K/n up to 0.30; beyond that root_rho warns.
  • A unit whose leverage in [X, B] exceeds 0.99 makes Δ ill-posed, and the fit is refused. Use fewer basis functions where the data are sparse, or the penalised smoother on an irregular region (see below).
  • A hat or B-spline basis sums to one, so do not also put an intercept in X.

β̂ is reported as a point estimate from least squares of y − ρ̂Wy on [X, B], without standard errors: the theory covers ρ only.

With a penalised smoother

A penalty lets the basis be large without spending one degree of freedom per column:

ps = tensor_pspline(u, v; k = 15)         # 225 hat functions and a difference penalty
r = root_rho(y, X, W; ps..., lambda = 0.1)
r.df                                      # tr S, the effective number of columns

The first stage is then S = Z(Z′Z + λΩ̃)⁻¹Z′, with X unpenalised, and M = I − S is not idempotent. Two things change.

  • The moment. It is the one-step exact-diagonal moment y₀′Ay₀, with A = MW + MDM and (M∘M)d = −diag(MW), so diag A = 0. Jin & Lee's two-step is not used. Its plug-in result needs Mf = 0, and a smoother shrinks f, so Mf ≠ 0. A is formed as a matrix: the shortcut through residual vectors, (My₀)′(MWy₀ + DMy₀), is a different moment when M² ≠ M, and it is biased.
  • The variance. The HC2 plug-in is ûᵢ²/(M²)ᵢᵢ, and the noise term uses H = (A + A′)S (THEORY.md, Proposition 4(c)).

The shrinkage is a bias. At the true ρ the moment's mean is f′Af, not zero. It grows with λ, and both intervals under-cover once c = f′Af/√V is not small: the test-inversion set covers at about P(|Z + c| ≤ 1.96). So choose λ small, smaller than a good fit of f needs. GCV and the RMSE-optimal λ were too large for valid inference in research/trace_cfe/tc_lambda.jl, which is why lambda has no default. Report ρ̂ across a few λ, as section 4 of examples/34_root_rho.jl does. Or use the bias-aware interval below, which stays valid at a larger λ. df/n above 0.30 gives a warning, and a unit with Sᵢᵢ above 0.99 is refused.

Irregular regions. tensor_pspline places its knots over the bounding box of the coordinates. When the units fill only part of it (a coastline, a border), some basis functions have no unit in their support, and others have only one or two. As a projection basis, these make [X, B] rank-deficient or put a unit's leverage at 1, and root_rho refuses the fit. The penalised smoother needs only Z′Z + λΩ̃ to be invertible: the penalty determines the coefficients that the data leave free. So on an irregular region, use the smoother:

ps = tensor_pspline(u, v; k = 12)   # some columns are zero at every unit
r = root_rho(y, X, W; ps..., lambda = 1.0)

On the 278 mainland Portuguese municipalities, the projection ran only at k = 4, while the smoother ran at k = 8 to 16 for every λ tried (research/trace_cfe/tc_portugal.jl). The bias-aware interval (with roughness) then uses the smoother itself as its pilot, as described below.

The bias-aware interval

Choosing λ small trades precision for validity. The bias-aware interval avoids that trade under one assumption: a bound ℛ on the roughness of f. Over the class {f = [X, B]ψ : ψ′Πψ ≤ ℛ²}, with Π the penalty, the shrinkage bias satisfies |f′Af| ≤ b̄ = ℛ√(v′Π⁺v) + ℛ²χ̄, where v = (MZ)′Wf is estimated from a pilot fit (the projection where it runs) and χ̄ bounds the quadratic part (THEORY.md, Lemma 4). The interval widens the critical value by that bound:

ℛ = basis_roughness(f_ref, ps.basis, ps.penalty; X)   # a surface at least as rough as f
r = root_rho(y, X, W; ps..., lambda = 1.0, roughness = ℛ)
r.ci_bias_aware                                       # ρ̂ ± cv(b̄/√V̂)·se
r = root_rho(y, X, W; ps..., lambda = 10.0 .^ (-3:0.5:2), roughness = ℛ)
r.lambda, r.lambda_grid, r.halflength_grid            # λ chosen by the interval's length

cv(t) is the level quantile of |N(t, 1)|. Its coverage holds uniformly over the class, at any λ (THEORY.md, Proposition 7). With a vector of lambdas, the projection is added as λ = 0 (bias bound 0), and the shortest candidate is returned. Proposition 7 covers that choice when one candidate is clearly the shortest. bonferroni = true covers any choice, at the price of a larger critical value.

ℛ is the user's assumption. The data cannot certify an upper bound on the roughness: a perturbation the data can barely see can be very rough. Every rule that estimated ℛ under-covered (0.45–0.77) in research/trace_cfe/tc_rchoice.jl. The loss is asymmetric, so err on the large side. A large ℛ moves the choice towards the projection and costs at most the gain; a small one costs coverage. In that design (n = 900, two amplitudes a of the surface; projection half-lengths 0.250 and 0.227):

ℛ / ℛ*0.51416

Here ℛ* is the true roughness. Report the result across a few ℛ, with, for each conclusion, the largest ℛ at which it holds.

When f is not in the span of the basis

The class above assumes f = [X, B]ψ, so the projection gets b̄ = 0. A real f leaves a remainder outside span(B), and that biases every candidate, the projection included. reference states the roughness class on a finer basis, while the estimator keeps B (THEORY.md, Lemma 5 and Proposition 7′):

ref = tensor_pspline(u, v; k = 30)                    # the class; K̃ may exceed n
ℛ̃ = basis_roughness((s, t) -> 2sin(2πs)cos(πt), u, v; k = 30)   # knot values, not least squares
r = root_rho(y, X, W; ps..., lambda = 10.0 .^ (-3:0.5:2), roughness = ℛ̃, reference = ref)
r = root_rho(y, X, W; basis = ps.basis, roughness = ℛ̃, reference = ref)   # the projection alone

The reference's unpenalised functions must be unpenalised in [X, B]: the same coordinates and the same penalty order. root_rho checks this. As the reference grid is refined, (k̃ − 1)·ℛ̃ settles, so the class is in effect a bound on the curvature of f.

In research/trace_cfe/tc_refbasis.jl (n = 900, f = x + a·sin(2πu)cos(πv)):

With ℛ̃ four times too large and 15 knots, the cost is 1.07× and 2.1×. So: use a fine B, and the reference to check that it is fine enough. Results across bases are comparable once the class is on the reference, and the shortest interval was at the finest B tried. The projection candidate (REsd) is covered by THEORY.md's Proposition 1′ when the largest remainder ‖Mf‖ in the class is small against √n.

Choosing the basis

With the class on the reference, the intervals of different bases bound the same thing, so the data can choose the basis too. Give basis (and penalty) as vectors, and the shortest interval over every (basis, λ) pair is returned (THEORY.md, Corollary 7′):

pss = [tensor_pspline(u, v; k) for k in (5, 10, 15)]
r = root_rho(y, X, W; basis = [p.basis for p in pss], penalty = [p.penalty for p in pss],
             lambda = 10.0 .^ (-3:0.5:2), roughness = ℛ̃, reference = ref)
r.basis_index, r.basis_grid      # the chosen basis, and the basis of each candidate

bonferroni = true counts every pair. Without reference this is refused: each basis would then have its own class, and a coarse basis would look short only because its class is small.

On an irregular region: the smoother as the pilot. The projection pilot needs [X, B] of full rank and every leverage below 0.99, which a tensor_pspline over a coastline or a border does not give. With pilot = :auto (the default), root_rho then uses the smoother at λ_p = pilot_lambda (by default the smallest lambda) as the pilot. It shrinks f too, and the bound adds two terms that depend on the design alone, ω_p and c̄_pℛ²r_p² (THEORY.md, Lemma 13 and Proposition 7″). r.pilot says which pilot was used.

ps = tensor_pspline(u, v; k = 24)
r = root_rho(y, X, W; ps..., lambda = [0.1, 1.0, 10.0, 100.0], roughness = ℛ)
r.pilot          # :penalised on an irregular region

In validation/mc_root_rho_pilot.jl (n = 900 on a triangle, 89 of 225 basis functions empty), the chosen interval covered 0.953 and 0.973 with ℛ = ℛ*, and 0.940 and 1.000 with ℛ = 4ℛ*; on the full square the two pilots gave intervals of the same length to within 0.3%. On the 2,882 Portuguese parishes the interval for the spatial lag is [0.31, 0.55] even with ℛ four times the roughness of the fitted surface (examples/34_root_rho.jl, section 8).

Cost

Everything is dense: about four n × n matrices. n is capped at max_dense_n = 4000 by default (about 0.5 GB); raise it if the memory is there. With the largest basis of the Monte Carlo (K/n ≈ 0.19), one replication (a root_rho fit plus a closed_form_rho fit) took 0.12 s at n = 900 and 0.35 s at n = 1600. The penalised smoother holds about six n × n matrices and solves one dense n × n system. One fit took 0.10 s at n = 900 (k = 15) and 0.29 s at n = 1600 (k = 20). A projection also computes vhat_worst_bias: two more n × n × n products, 0.04 s at n = 900 and 1.8 s at n = 3969.

What is proved and what is measured

Jin & Lee prove consistency and asymptotic normality for a design of fixed dimension. The results for a basis that grows with n are in research/trace_cfe/THEORY.md: a CLT for the moment with a dense M (Theorem 1), the first step's plug-in having no first-order effect (Proposition 1), the consistency of HC2 V̂ (Proposition 2), and the validity of CS without identification (Proposition 6), and its coverage when K/n does not tend to 0, with the worst case of V̂'s bias (Proposition 6″, Lemmas 7–10). The penalised smoother, its moment and its V̂ are Proposition 4. No one else has checked these proofs.

What the Monte Carlo shows: the projection

validation/mc_root_rho.jl uses 108 cells of 1000 replications each. The cells cross:

  • W: kNN-6, rook, kNN-20;
  • n = 400, 900, 1600;
  • three basis sizes with K/n ≈ 0.05, 0.11, 0.19;
  • smooth or rough variances;
  • normal or standardised χ²₃ errors.

Throughout, ρ₀ = 0.4. The Monte Carlo SE of a coverage near 0.95 is 0.007.

  • The plain closed form fails. Its bias grows with K/n and with the density of W. At kNN-20 with K/n = 0.19 it is about −1.7: the estimate sits at the lower bound.

  • root_rho stays close to unbiased. Its largest bias, −0.06, is in the weakest cell: kNN-20 at n = 400 with K = 82, where the sd of ρ̂ is 0.30.

  • What the weak cells look like. In the kNN-20 cells at n = 400:

    • up to 71 of 1000 samples had no admissible root;
    • CS reached a bound of rho_bounds in up to 740 of 1000 samples, and split into two intervals in up to 17;
    • CS still covered at 0.935–0.967.

    In these cells CS gives a wide set where the data say little about ρ. In every other cell, CS and the Wald interval agree closely.

What the Monte Carlo shows: the smoother

validation/mc_root_rho_smoother.jl uses 108 cells of 1000 replications each. The cells cross:

  • W: kNN-6, rook, kNN-20;
  • n = 400, 900, 1600, with k = 10, 15, 20 knots a side (k²/n = 0.25);
  • λ = 0.01, 0.1, 1;
  • f = x + a·sin(2πu)cos(πv) with a = 1 or 4;
  • smooth variances with normal errors, or rough variances with standardised χ²₃ errors.

Throughout, ρ₀ = 0.4. In each cell the size of the shrinkage bias, c = f′Af/√V, is computed exactly, and with it the predicted coverage P(|Z + c| ≤ 1.96). The pass criterion, set before the run: in every cell with c ≤ 0.25, both intervals cover within 0.93–0.99. In the other cells the coverage is compared with the prediction.

Results (validation/mc_root_rho_smoother_output.txt, 310 minutes). The pass criterion holds:

  • c ≤ 0.25 (75 of 108 cells): the Wald interval covers 0.935–0.982 and CS 0.934–0.982, and |bias| of ρ̂ is at most 0.045. This includes the weakly identified kNN-20 cells at n = 400, where up to 62 of 1000 draws have no root and CS still covers 0.929–0.964.
  • c > 0.25 (33 cells): the coverage falls as predicted. CS is within 0.027 of P(|Z + c| ≤ 1.96) in every cell, and the Wald interval within −0.007 to +0.077 of it. At a = 4, λ = 1, c reaches 8.2 and both intervals cover almost never: the bias is f′Af, not noise.
  • What sets c: with a = 1, every cell at λ ≤ 0.1 has c ≤ 0.13; with a = 4, λ = 0.01 keeps c ≤ 0.33, but λ = 0.1 already gives up to 1.8. So choose λ small enough that r.df stays well above the smoothness of f, and report results across a few λ. There is no rule for choosing λ yet.
  • Power: the residual-vector shortcut, on the same draws, is biased by −0.015 to −0.244 in the c ≤ 0.25 cells, and its Wald interval covers below 0.93 in 48 of them (down to 0.512).
  • The package against the dense reference: ρ̂ equal to 1.6e-14 and the SE to 6.4e-16, on the first draw of every cell.
