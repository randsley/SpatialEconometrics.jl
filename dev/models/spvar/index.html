Spatial VAR

fit_star models one variable through space and time. A regional economist usually has several, and wants to ask whether a shock to one of them in region i moves another one in region j, and after how long. That is a system question, and answering it honestly means taking a position on contemporaneous causal ordering — which is the one thing a spatial panel cannot settle from the data.

This page covers SpVAR, an implementation of Di Giacinto (2010), On vector autoregressive modeling in space and time, Journal of Geographical Systems 12(2), 125–154.

The specification

Stack the data location-within-variable, yₜ = [y₁₁ₜ,…,y_N₁ₜ,…,y₁Kₜ,…,y_NKₜ]′, length NK. The structural form is

C₀ yₜ = μ + C₁ yₜ₋₁ + … + C_p yₜ₋ₚ + uₜ,   E(uₜuₜ′) = Λ = diag(λ₁₁,…,λ_NK)

Each C_h is partitioned into K×K blocks A⁽ʰ⁾_kr of size N×N, and each block is a polynomial in the order-l spatial weights,

A⁽ʰ⁾_kr = Σ_{l=0}^{s} W⁽ʰˡ⁾_kr Φ⁽ʰˡ⁾_kr

with Φ diagonal in the locations. SpVAR ships the nested homogeneous case, Φ⁽ʰˡ⁾_kr = φ⁽ʰˡ⁾_kr I_N.

Block triangularity, and why it is not strict triangularity

A conventional SVAR identifies C₀ by making it triangular. Doing that here would order not only the variables but the locations: a recursive causal chain through space, so that region 3 may affect region 7 contemporaneously but never the reverse. Spatial interaction is bilateral, so Di Giacinto rejects that and makes C₀ triangular in the variable index only, with full N×N blocks on the diagonal:

A⁽⁰⁾_kk = I_N − Σ_{l=1}^{s} W⁽⁰ˡ⁾_kk Φ⁽ˡ⁾_kk      (the SAR-form diagonal block)
A⁽⁰⁾_kr = −Σ_{l=0}^{s} W⁽⁰ˡ⁾_kr Φ_kr              for r < k

Two consequences, both load-bearing.

The parameter count becomes linear in N. Each C_h holds NK²(s+1) free coefficients instead of (NK)².

The likelihood separates. Block triangularity gives |C₀| = Π_r |A⁽⁰⁾_rr|, so concentrating Λ out of the FIML log-likelihood leaves a sum of K unrelated terms, one per endogenous variable:

L̃ = Σ_{k=1}^{K} L̃_k,   L̃_k = c̃_k + T log|A⁽⁰⁾_kk| − (T/2) Σ_i log λ̂_ik

A spatial VAR is therefore K spatial autoregressions, not one NK×NK problem. fit_spvar maximises each L̃_k on its own. Both identities are tested to machine precision (0.000e+00 for the determinant factorisation and 0.000e+00 for the separability, testsets 40c and 40d).

The asymptotics run the other way

This is a `T → ∞` estimator at fixed `N`

Every other panel estimator in this package is consistent as N grows. Di Giacinto's FIML is not: its asymptotics are standard SVAR asymptotics, T → ∞ with N held fixed. A wide short panel is the wrong shape of data for this model.

That is a claim, so it was measured. The bias in a dynamic coefficient (g(t−1), truth 0.30), from a homogeneous SpVAR(1,1) with K = 2 on a ring:

The ordering is required, and it changes the answer

ordering has no default. It names the contemporaneous causal order of the K variables, first mover first, and it is the identifying assumption. Defaulting it would hide the only choice the user has to make.

On a 14-region ring with T = 400 (examples/29_spvar.jl, sections 2 and 3), reversing the ordering of a two-variable system moved the log-likelihood by 9.949 and the peak outward STIR of y to a shock in g by a factor of 5.25 at spatial order 0 and 1.33 at order 1.

It also moved the timing. Under the true ordering the order-0 response peaks on impact, h = 0, because the shock reaches y within the period. Under the reversed ordering it peaks at h = 2, because the effect now has to travel through a lag to exist at all. The reversed fit does not re-label the shocks; it answers a different question, and both the size and the date of its answer are wrong.

Impulse responses in space and time

spvar_irf returns the raw structural response matrices Ψ_h = Ψ̃_h C₀⁻¹, where Ψ̃_i = Σ_{h=1}^{i} Ψ̃_{i−h} C̃_h and Ψ̃₀ = I, so Ψ⁽ʰ⁾_kr(i,j) = ∂y_{ik,t+h}/∂u_{jrt}. Summing them reproduces the long-run multiplier (C₀ − Σ_h C_h)⁻¹ to 2.665e-15.

space_time_stir aggregates those NK×NK matrices into the paper's space-time impulse responses, which come in two directions:

  • outward, ζ⁽ʰˡ⁾_kr(i) = Σ_j w⁽ˡ⁾_ij Ψ⁽ʰ⁾_kr(j,i) — my shock reaching my order-l neighbours;
  • inward, ξ⁽ʰˡ⁾_kr(i) = Σ_j w⁽ˡ⁾_ij Ψ⁽ʰ⁾_kr(i,j) — their shocks reaching me.

The two differ by a transpose, which is the whole content of the distinction. They coincide when K = 1 and the W⁽ˡ⁾ are symmetric, and that is a free exactness check: measured at 5.551e-17 on an unstandardised path and 1.388e-17 on a ring.

Row standardisation alone makes outward and inward differ

On an irregular graph, row standardising W destroys its symmetry, and order_weights_list row standardises by default. Measured on a 14-unit path with K = 1: a W asymmetry of 0.5 produced an outward/inward STIR gap of 1.345e-02, against 2.776e-17 for the same path spectrally standardised, where the asymmetry is exactly zero.

So the gap is not only a multivariate effect: under row standardisation a unit with fewer neighbours sends and receives on different scales, and the split reports that. If you want the two to coincide, use spectral_standardize, which bounds the spectrum without breaking symmetry. Leaving the weights raw is not the alternative — a binary path has eigenvalues near 2, so a model that is stationary under standardised weights is explosive under raw ones.

Testing for spatial interaction at all

Setting every spatial coefficient to zero collapses the SpVAR into a nested panel VAR, one small VAR per location,

M₀,ᵢ y_it = μᵢ + Σ_h M_h,ᵢ y_i,t−h + u_it

with M₀,ᵢ unit lower triangular. Di Giacinto gives this as a reason to prefer block triangularity: the nested model stays identified even where the SpVAR itself does not. It also gives a likelihood-ratio test.

fit_nested_panel_var is written independently of fit_spvar, which is what makes the reduction worth testing: fit_spvar(…; s = 0) reproduces it to 0.000e+00 in both the log-likelihood and every coefficient (testset 40e).

lr_spatial_interaction is that LR test, on s(K(K+1)/2 + K²p) degrees of freedom. Its null distribution was simulated rather than assumed, on data generated with no spatial interaction at all:

rejection rate under the null10%5%1%
N = 12, T = 1000.11000.05270.0113
N = 12, T = 4000.11470.05130.0080
N = 24, T = 2000.11930.06200.0100

1,500 replications, so the Monte Carlo standard error is about 0.008 at the 10% level and 0.006 at 5%. The test is mildly liberal at 10%, by one to two standard errors, and close to nominal at 5% and 1%. Power against the spatial DGP is 1.000 at the 5% level already at T = 50.

150 replications said the opposite

An earlier run at 150 replications reported the test conservative (10%: 0.073–0.087, 5%: 0.033–0.047, 1%: 0.000–0.007). That was noise: the Monte Carlo standard error at 150 draws is 0.024 at the 10% level, wide enough to swallow the whole finding and reverse its sign. A size table is not worth printing until the replication count is large enough for the error bar to be smaller than the deviation being claimed.

`homogeneous = true` is the comparable case

The heterogeneous nested panel VAR has more parameters than the homogeneous SpVAR, not fewer, so it is not nested in it and must not be LR-tested against it. lr_spatial_interaction fits the homogeneous restriction.

At K = 1, and what it is not comparable to

With one variable the system collapses to a single spatial autoregression with its own lags, (I − ρW)yₜ = μ + (φ₀I + φ₁W)yₜ₋₁ + uₜ, and |C₀| reduces to |I − ρW|. That is a specification fit_star cannot express, because fit_star has no contemporaneous Wyₜ by construction.

It is not the same model as fit_dynamic_spatial_panel, and the two should not be compared coefficient by coefficient.

  • Handing fit_dynamic_spatial_panel a column of y(t−1) refuses with an identification error, because it builds its own time lag and the column is exactly collinear with it.
  • Fitted as intended on data from the specification above, it returns ρ̂ = 0.1875 where fit_spvar returns 0.3749 against a true ρ = 0.40. Neither is in error: the dynamic spatial panel has no W yₜ₋₁ term, and omitting one that is present does not leave ρ alone — it roughly halves it, since that spatial content has nowhere to go but the contemporaneous spatial parameter.
  • fit_spvar also estimates a per-unit variance λᵢ; the dynamic spatial panel assumes a common one.
How `μ` is absorbed, and why `s = 0` is not exact to machine precision

fit_spvar demeans each unit's level series once, over t = p+1 … T. This is required rather than convenient: the spatial lag of a demeaned series is not the demeaned spatial lag, so demeaning the constructed columns separately would break (I − ρW)ỹ and the Jacobian with it.

At s = 0 there is no spatial term and the exact profiled likelihood demeans the lagged and current sub-series by their own means instead, so the two differ in finite samples. The difference is second order in T, measured against an independently written profiled FIML: 1.45e-05 at T = 300 falling to 1.11e-06 at T = 1200 and 5.06e-08 at T = 4800 — a ratio of about 4 per doubling, so T²·gap is roughly constant where T·gap is not.

The order condition

Counting equations against free coefficients in C₀ gives

NK(NK − 1) ≥ NK(K − 1) + sNK(K + 1)   ⟺   N ≥ s + (s + K)/K

so K = 2, s = 1 needs N ≥ 3 and K = 2, s = 2 needs N ≥ 4. spvar_order_condition returns that verdict, and fit_spvar refuses before estimating anything when it fails, reporting the bound and the counts that produced it. Returning numbers for an under-identified system is not a service.

It is an order condition: necessary, not sufficient. A model that satisfies it can still be unidentified in a particular sample, and check_design_rank is the second line of defence.

The bound above counts the heterogeneous coefficients, which is the condition the paper prints. fit_spvar estimates the homogeneous model, so it applies the homogeneous count — refusing on the heterogeneous bound would refuse models it can in fact estimate. Measured at K = 2, s = 2 on a ring, 60 fits each: N = 4, 5, 8 all estimate without incident (60/60, mean companion spectral radius 0.711 to 0.727), and N = 3 satisfies the homogeneous condition but is refused one step earlier — by order_weights_list, because a 3-ring has diameter 1 and W⁽²⁾ is empty. The guards bind in the right order and for the right reason.

The weights are order-l, not powers of W

W⁽ˡ⁾ means the matrix of order-l neighbours in the sense of Anselin and Smirnov (1996): pairs at graph distance exactly l. W^l is not that. Powers count walks, so they are non-zero on the diagonal and double-count paths, and where the graph has triangles they retain first-order neighbours at order 2.

order_weights and order_weights_list (in SpatialWeights) build them by breadth-first shortest-path hop count. On a 4×4 rook lattice with 48 first-order links, W² has 84 non-zeros, 16 of them on the diagonal, against 68 and 0 for order_weights(W, 2); summed over orders 1 to 6 the order matrices cover exactly N(N−1) = 240 ordered pairs, each once.

order_weights_list refuses when an order is empty rather than fitting a structurally zero column, and reports the graph diameter so you can lower s.

Forecasting, and what to compare it against

spvar_forecast(m, h; y_history) iterates the reduced form C̃_h = C₀⁻¹C_h forward and adds the absorbed per-unit intercepts back, so it returns forecasts in levels. y_history is required: the model stores no data.

Two things to get right when judging the result.

Benchmark against each unit's in-sample mean, not against zero. Because the intercepts are absorbed during estimation and restored here, a zero benchmark says nothing unless the series happen to be centred — and it cannot detect a dropped intercept, which is the defect most worth ruling out. On a system with intercepts of order 50 and −30, spvar_forecast beats the sample-mean forecast by 7.5× and 4.4× in RMSE; against zero it would appear to beat it by 200×, which measures the intercepts rather than the model.

Read the error by horizon. The forecastable part decays at the companion spectral radius. On a stationary system with radius 0.74 (examples/29_spvar.jl, section 8) the gain over the sample mean is clear at h = 1 — 1.1978 against 1.4670 — essentially gone by h = 2, and at h = 3 the model is marginally worse. Averaged over twelve horizons the same forecast reads as a 1.03× improvement, which looks like a failure and is not one: it is the model doing what the spectral radius permits. Check m.spectral_radius before deciding what horizon to expect anything at.

Computational notes

  • s = 1 makes each diagonal block the familiar I − ρW, so the Chebyshev log-determinant approximation applies.
  • s ≥ 2 makes it I − Σ_l φ_l W⁽ˡ⁾, a linear combination of several weight matrices. chebyshev_logdet is single-parameter by construction and does not apply; SpVAR uses an exact LU there. This is a documented limitation, not an oversight.
  • The companion matrix is Kp·N square. SpVARModel reports its spectral radius and stationary flag; a non-stationary fit warns rather than returning silently divergent impulse responses.

What is not here

  • Bootstrap confidence bands for the STIR (the paper's §5 tail). The point estimates and asymptotic intervals stand without them.
  • Spatially heterogeneous coefficients — the full eq (7), with Φ genuinely diagonal rather than scalar. The global STIR summaries assume homogeneity, and moderate N cannot support the heterogeneous count. Note that the order condition's counts are the heterogeneous ones, so the guard is implemented for both regardless.
  • Time-varying ρ, which was once filed beside this row. See the roadmap: it belongs with the partially linear spatial model, since local-linear concentrated QML and profile QML share one kernel-weighted likelihood.
