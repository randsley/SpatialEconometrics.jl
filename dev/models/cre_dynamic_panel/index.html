Short-T Dynamic Panel with Correlated Random Effects

fit_dynamic_spatial_panel corrects the incidental-parameter bias of a dynamic spatial panel with the Yu, de Jong & Lee device, which needs a long T. Many panels are short: a handful of years across many regions or firms. For those, fit_cre_dynamic_spatial_panel implements Li & Yang's (2021) M-estimator, which is consistent with T fixed and n → ∞. This page keeps Li & Yang's symbols, since the coefficient names follow them: n is the number of units and ρ the time lag (see Notation).

The model

\[y_t = ρy_{t-1} + λ_1W_1y_t + λ_2W_2y_{t-1} + X_tβ + Zγ + μ + α_t1_n + u_t,\quad u_t = λ_3W_3u_t + v_t,\]

for t = 1, …, T, with the individual effects correlated random effects in Mundlak's form, μ = X̄π + ε, where X̄ is the mean of X_0, …, X_T. The time effects α_t enter as period dummies. W_2 (the space-time lag) and W_3 (the spatial error) are optional: pass nothing to drop λ₂ or λ₃ together with its estimating equation.

Mundlak's device is what lets time-invariant regressors Z stay in the model: under fixed effects they would be swept out with μ.

Why m is required

The panel is observed from period 0, but the process started earlier. Li & Yang assume it started m periods before period 0 from an exogenous y_{−m}. Then y_0 carries ε, and with T fixed the conditional QMLE, which treats y_0 as given, is inconsistent.

The M-estimator corrects each biased component of the conditional score by its expectation (Li & Yang's Lemma 2.1). The corrections involve y_0 only through m. So m is a required keyword with no default:

fit = fit_cre_dynamic_spatial_panel(y, X, Z, W1, N, T + 1; m = 10,
                                    W2 = W2, W3 = W3, compute_cqmle = true)
  • m = 0 says y_0 is exogenous. The conditional likelihood is then exact, the corrections vanish, and the M-estimator is the conditional QMLE. The test suite checks this identity.
  • For a stationary process, 𝓑ᵐ = (B₁⁻¹B₂)ᵐ dies out geometrically, so any large m gives nearly the same corrections. Li & Yang report robustness to a wrong m in that sense. A small m is a different assumption about y_0. In example 31, m = 2 moves ρ̂ by .003, a tenth of its standard error.

Data layout

As in fit_dynamic_spatial_panel: y and X stack n units (the argument N) within each of T_total = T + 1 periods, period 0 first. Period 0 supplies y_0 and enters X̄; estimation uses periods 1..T. Z is an n × q matrix of time-invariant regressors without an intercept, or nothing.

A column of X that does not vary over time duplicates its own mean X̄. The fit refuses that design as rank deficient. Put such a variable in Z.

Estimation

β and σ²_v are concentrated out, and the adjusted quasi-score is solved in δ = (φ, ρ, λ₁[, λ₂][, λ₃]), where φ = σ²_ε/σ²_v. No nT × nT matrix is formed. Ω⁻¹ and log|Ω| have closed forms through J̄_T = J_T/T, and the corrections are traces of n × n products, through powers of 𝓑.

Two points about solving it.

  • The adjusted score can have more than one root. Started from the conditional QMLE, the solver can find a root near it, not near the truth. The start is therefore 2SLS on first differences, which removes μ. The instruments are y_{t−2} with its spatial lags, ΔX_t, ΔX_{t−1} and their spatial lags. φ and λ₃ start from moment estimates on the residuals.
  • φ = ∞ is a false root. As φ grows, Ω⁻¹ loses its between-unit part and the φ score tends to zero, so a solver can drift towards φ = ∞. The solver therefore works with the score in log φ, which tends to −n/2, by Levenberg–Marquardt in (log φ, ρ, λ).

A poor start for φ can still lead the solver towards φ = ∞. If the solver does not reach a root, it is run once more from the same start with φ = 1. If that fails too, the fit throws: it never returns a non-root.

Standard errors

The covariance is the sandwich Ĵ⁻¹Γ̂Ĵ⁻¹', with Ĵ the Jacobian of the adjusted score and Γ̂ an estimate of Var(S*(ψ₀)).

Γ̂ is not Li & Yang's §3 hybrid estimator. It adapts the corrected plug-in route of Li, Meng & Yang (2026), written for the fixed-effects version of this model:

  1. Split y_0 = η + C₀ε, where η is independent of the in-sample shocks u = (ε; v_1; …; v_T). Given η, every score component is u'Au + a(η)'u + c, so Γ = E Var(S*|η) has a closed form in the shock variances and third and fourth moments.
  2. η is unobserved. It is replaced by its plug-in through ε̂ = E[ε|e], and the bias that plug-in adds, a trace in Var(ε − ε̂), is subtracted.
  3. The third and fourth moments of ε and v are estimated from the within and between parts of the residuals.

At the true parameters and moments, Γ̂ is unbiased for the exact Var(S*). The test suite checks this against a brute-force covariance of the linear-quadratic forms over all shocks, pre-sample ones included. Without the bias correction in step 2, the same check fails.

vcov_hessian keeps Ĵ⁻¹ for comparison. It is not a valid covariance when m > 0, because the adjusted score is not an information-equal score.

Assumptions. The errors may be non-normal but must be homoskedastic. Li & Yang's Supplementary Appendix (§II) shows that heteroskedasticity in ε or v enters the score corrections themselves, so it breaks the estimator and not only the variance.

Cost. The estimator needs dense n × n algebra. Γ̂ forms dense n(T+1) × n(T+1) matrices, one for each of σ², φ, ρ and the λs, so its memory grows as (n(T+1))²: by count, about 1.4 GB at n = 1000, T = 3 before temporaries (not measured).

Monte Carlo

validation/mc_cre_sdpd.jl replicates Li & Yang's Table 1 design: DGP1, T = 3, m = 10, queen W₁ = W₃, group-interaction W₂, X_t as in their footnote 7. It has 1000 replications at n = 100 and 500 at n = 200, and normal, normal-mixture and centred χ²₃ errors. The output is validation/mc_cre_sdpd_output.txt.   • The CQMLE does not improve with n. Its ρ̂ stays at .351, and π̂ and φ̂ stay low. The M-estimator removes these biases at both sizes, and its means agree with Li & Yang's to within about one Monte Carlo standard error.
  • λ₃ stays biased down at T = 3, by .055 at n = 100 and .023 at n = 200, as in Li & Yang's table. The M-estimator does not correct it.
  • Coverage of the 95% sandwich intervals is between .92 and .97 for β, ρ and the λs in all six cells. For σ²_v and φ it is .89–.93 at n = 100 and .92–.94 at n = 200: these are the slowest to reach their asymptotics, and Li & Yang's own robust SEs are low for them by a similar amount.
  • The normality-based SEs fail under non-normal errors. With χ²₃ errors, the intervals from vcov_hessian cover σ²_v 79% of the time at n = 100 and 81% at n = 200: more data does not help. The sandwich covers 89% and 94%.

All 4500 fits succeeded. An earlier run lost 5, each with an error rather than a wrong estimate; docs/AUDIT.md gives the causes, the fixes and the full table.
