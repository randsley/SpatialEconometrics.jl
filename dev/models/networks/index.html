Spatial Econometrics for Networks

Everywhere else in this package W comes from distance. Here it comes from a graph — trade links, friendships, supply chains, citations — and one thing changes that matters more than the construction does.

On a map, a unit's neighbours and its neighbours' neighbours are different sets, because distance decays. On a network that is not automatic, and when it fails the peer-effects model is not identified: the response to peers' outcomes and the response to peers' characteristics cannot be told apart at all. Bramoullé, Djebbari and Fortin (2009) make this precise, and the failure lands on the structure applied work reaches for first.

So the first function here is not an estimator.

The reflection problem, mechanically

The linear-in-means model, their eq. (1):

y = αι + βGy + γx + δGx + ε

β is the endogenous effect — the response to what peers do — and δ the contextual effect — the response to who peers are. Separating them is the whole point, and it is the thing that fails.

Their Proposition 1: social effects are identified if I, G and G² are linearly independent; if those are linearly dependent and no individual is isolated, social effects are not identified. Their footnote 17 gives the check, which is what peer_identification runs: vectorize each matrix, stack the columns, and take the rank of the result.

With network fixed effects — an unobservable correlated with x at the network level, swept out by the within-local transformation (I - G) — their Proposition 4 requires I, G, G², G³ to be independent instead. Strictly more demanding, because, in their words, information has been lost to take into account the presence of correlated effects.

What fails, and what does not

Every verdict here is pinned as a test, and the algebraic identities behind them hold to machine precision because they are identities in the paper.

Interaction structureProp. 1Prop. 4Why
Equal-sized groups✗✗rank = 2. Manski's reflection problem
Groups, mean includes self✗✗G² = G exactly, for any sizes
Two distinct group sizes✓✗G³ is an exact combination of I, G, G²
Three distinct group sizes✓✓the paper's "three classroom sizes are now necessary"
Star / complete bipartite✓✗G³ = G exactly
Line, cycle, random graph✓✓intransitive triads; diameter ≥ 3 suffices (their Corollary 1)

The second row is worth dwelling on. Taking the group mean over everyone including the unit itself — the obvious way to compute a classroom average — makes G² = G identically, and no amount of variation in group size rescues it. Switching to the leave-one-out mean is the difference between a model that is identified and one that never can be.

peer_identification(group_weights([5, 5, 5]))                      # not identified
peer_identification(group_weights([4, 5, 6]))                      # identified
peer_identification(group_weights([4, 5, 6]; include_self = true)) # not identified
peer_identification(group_weights([4, 6]); fixed_effects = true)   # not identified

The tolerance is measured

Rank decisions need a tolerance, and this one was set by measurement rather than convention. Across the degenerate structures above the largest spurious singular value observed was 1.97e-8; across identified structures the smallest genuine one was 4.0e-3. The default tol = 1e-6 sits between them with two orders of magnitude of margin each way.

An earlier default of 1e-8 sat below that noise floor and reported two equal groups of 8 as identified, contradicting Proposition 2 outright. The gap field reports where a given network fell, so a verdict resting on the tolerance is visible rather than implied.

Estimation

fit_peer_effects runs the check first and throws when it fails, because what it would otherwise return is two coefficients whose split the data does not determine — printed with ordinary-looking standard errors and stars. check = false overrides it, explicitly.

Estimation is 2SLS with the paper's own instruments: the characteristics of peers' peers, G²x, which enter the reduced form but not the structural equation. Under fixed effects everything is transformed by (I - G) first and the instrument block is (I-G)G²x, exactly as their §4 writes it.

G   = network_weights(adjacency)
fit = fit_peer_effects(y, X, G)
fit.beta            # endogenous peer effect
fit.first_stage_F   # whether this sample can exploit the identification

Identified is not estimable

The rank condition is a statement about the population. Whether a given sample can exploit it is separate, and on a dense network the answer is no: a unit's peers' peers are nearly everybody, so G²x flattens towards a constant.

This reproduces Bramoullé, Djebbari and Fortin's own simulation finding that precision is a decreasing function of density. The spread of β̂ rises twenty-one-fold across the range and the median biases toward zero, while the first-stage F collapses from 36.5 to 4.4.

Note the last column. The rejection rate does not deteriorate — it improves. The standard errors grow faster than the estimates do, so the test becomes conservative exactly as the estimate becomes useless. Nothing in the significance of the result will tell you this is happening, which is why first_stage_F is reported and warned on.

Covariance

The default is :robust. Bramoullé, Djebbari and Fortin leave the error variance matrix unrestricted and explicitly do not impose homoskedasticity, and the measurement supports the default — but only under heteroskedasticity strong enough to matter. Coverage of nominal 95% intervals for β, 2,000 replications, n = 300:

βErrors:robust:homoskedastic
0.0homoskedastic0.9660.970
0.0heteroskedastic0.9700.942
0.3heteroskedastic0.9640.926
0.6heteroskedastic0.9610.921

Two things to take from this. The gap widens with β: the spatial multiplier (I - βG)⁻¹ compounds the heteroskedasticity across the network rather than averaging it away. And mild heteroskedasticity — a 1.5× spread in the error standard deviation — does not separate the two estimators at all; the table above uses an 11× spread. So the default is the right one and will often make no difference.

Under fixed effects, vcov = :transform accounts for the correlation the within-local transformation itself induces, since Var((I-G)ε) = σ²(I-G)(I-G)' is not scalar even when ε is.

Over-identification

With K characteristics and one endogenous regressor there are K - 1 over-identifying restrictions, so the Sargan test exists once K ≥ 2. It is well sized throughout, 2,000 replications:

Several channels at once

Units are often linked more than one way. fit_multiplex_sar fits

y = Σⱼ ρⱼ Wⱼ y + Xβ + ε

giving each channel its own ρⱼ, which a single pooled W cannot report. Estimated by concentrated maximum likelihood, with β and σ² concentrated out and the J-dimensional profile maximised by coordinate ascent using the same Brent search the single-channel fit uses.

log|I - Σρⱼ Wⱼ| is computed exactly by sparse LU at every evaluation. The Chebyshev approximation available for a single W rests on precomputed traces of its powers and does not carry over to an arbitrary combination, so it is not offered here rather than offered and quietly wrong.

With one channel this reproduces fit_spatial(SAR, …) — log-likelihood to 1e-13, standard errors to 1e-9 — against independently written, already audited code, which is what validates the generalised information matrix.

Channels that are collinear as matrices cannot have their ρs told apart, for the same reason I, G, G² must be independent above, and the fit refuses rather than returning an arbitrary split. The parameter space is Σⱼ|ρⱼ| < 1, sufficient for stationarity when each Wⱼ is row-standardised; a fit pinned against that boundary is reported with converged = false.

Isolates

Social networks have units with no links; geographic weight matrices rarely do. Row standardisation leaves those rows all-zero rather than producing NaN, and peer_identification reports the count — because Proposition 1's non-identification half is stated only for networks without isolates. When the rank is short and isolates are present the verdict is reported as a failure to establish identification rather than as a proof of its absence, and conclusive is false.
