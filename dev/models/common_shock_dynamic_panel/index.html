Short-T Dynamic Panel with Common Shocks

fit_cre_dynamic_spatial_panel handles a short dynamic spatial panel whose individual effects are time-invariant. Often the units also respond, each in its own way, to shocks that hit them all at once: a recession, a policy change, a commodity price. fit_common_shock_dynamic_panel implements Bai & Chen's (2026) conditional QML for that case, with T fixed and N → ∞.

A preprint

Bai & Chen (2026) is an arXiv preprint (2608.22499v1), not yet refereed. The package checks its formulas against independent computations (below), but the paper's theory is as stated by its authors.

The model

\[y_t = ρWy_t + φy_{t-1} + X_tβ + 1_Nδ_t + Λf_t + ε_t, \quad t = 1, …, T,\]

with r common factors f_t and unit loadings Λ = (λ_1, …, λ_N)'. The time effects δ_t are free, so X has no intercept. The idiosyncratic errors ε_it are independent across units and over time, with variances σ²_t that may change over time but not across units.

With T fixed, the N loadings cannot be estimated one by one: that is the incidental-parameter problem behind Nickell bias. Bai & Chen project them on observed controls instead:

\[λ_i = Az_i + η_i, \quad z_i = [(Q_X)_i, (WQ_X)_i, …, (W^LQ_X)_i, (y_0)_i, (Wy_0)_i, …, (W^Ly_0)_i],\]

the spatially enriched random loadings (SERL). Q_X summarises each unit's regressors (by default its mean of X over periods 1..T), and L is the SERL order. The residual loadings η_i have mean zero and a common covariance Σ_η given the data. Only A, Σ_η and the factor path F = (f_1, …, f_T)' are estimated, never a parameter per unit.

F is identified up to rotation, so it is normalised to F = [I_r; F_2]. Its top r × r block must be full rank. Bai & Chen's Remark 3 (a constant first factor for time-invariant heterogeneity) is not implemented.

What L assumes

The likelihood is conditional on y_0. It is right only when E(λ_i | y_0, X, W) = Az_i for the chosen L (Bai & Chen's Assumption 2).

If y_0 was itself produced by a long-running spatial process, it carries the neighbours' loadings through (I − ρW)⁻¹, and no finite L captures that exactly. The paper says so (§2.5): a finite L is then an approximation. Its Table 4 measures the cost at T = 5, ρ = 0.5:

Ncoverage of ρ, L = 0coverage of ρ, L = 1
500.642.918
1000.312.935

At L = 0 coverage falls as N grows, the signature of an inconsistent estimator. At L = 1 it does not fall between the two sizes, but two sizes cannot show that it never will. This package's Monte Carlo (below) carries the comparison to N = 4000.

The same assumption applies to Q_X: the default unit mean of X can leave part of λ_i behind when the regressors are serially correlated with the loadings. The Monte Carlo below measures that case too.

L defaults to 1. r is required and has no default: the theory takes it as known and gives no rule for choosing it.

Data layout

As in fit_dynamic_spatial_panel: y and X stack N units within each of T_total = T + 1 periods, period 0 first. Period 0 supplies y_0; X's period-0 rows are not used. Q replaces the default Q_X, and controls replaces the whole of z_i. The controls are centred and scaled before estimation, so A is reported in standard-deviation units.

ev = eigvals(Matrix(W))   # once per W
fit = fit_common_shock_dynamic_panel(y, X, W, N, T + 1; r = 2, L = 1,
                                     eigenvalues = ev)

Estimation

The criterion is the Gaussian quasi log-likelihood conditional on y_0, eq. (17). Its only Jacobian term is T log|I − ρW|, evaluated from the eigenvalues of W. They are computed once per fit, at O(N³) cost, or passed in with eigenvalues when many panels share one W.

  1. Start (Appendix C): per-period OLS at ρ = 0, then principal components of the residuals for F and the loadings.
  2. Algorithm 1: given ρ, alternate the closed-form updates of δ and A, the ECME updates of Σ_η, σ²_t and F, and GLS for (φ, β); then maximise over ρ with the rest held fixed, and repeat.
  3. Newton polish: Newton steps on the full parameter vector, with step halving, until the largest |∂ℓ/∂α|/N is below tol (default 1e-8). If that is not reached, the fit throws: it never returns a point that does not solve the first-order conditions.

Standard errors

The score of ρ (and of φ) for one unit depends on the other units' errors through (I − ρW)⁻¹, so the unit scores are not independent, and their plain outer product misses cross-unit terms. Bai & Chen rearrange each pair term onto the later unit of the pair, which makes the contributions g_i a martingale-difference array (Appendix D). The covariance of the score is then estimated by their eq. (54), quoted in their notation:

\[\widehat Ω_M(α) := \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} g^†_i(α)\,g^†_i(α)' + \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j<i} v_{ij}(α)\,v_{ij}(α)'.\]

Their α is the full parameter vector. The estimator is the one-unit parts' outer products plus the outer products of every pair term. The pair terms are symmetric in i and j, so Ω̂_M does not depend on the order of the units, although each g_i does.

The reported vcov is the θ = (ρ, φ, β) block of the sandwich Ĥ⁻¹Ω̂Ĥ⁻¹, with the pair part scaled by N/(N − p) (eq. 23, the paper's preferred version; p counts all parameters). vcov_uncorrected leaves the factor out. The θ block equals the profiled covariance of §3.4. Both allow non-normal η_i and ε_i.

Cost. The pair part involves all N(N−1)/2 pairs. Because the blocks of (I_N⊗R_T(φ) − ρW⊗I_T)⁻¹ are φᵏ(I − ρW)^{−(k+1)}, the pair terms come out of N × N matrix products with no loop over pairs, in O(TN³) time and about eight N × N matrices of memory: about 1 GB at N = 4000, by count, not measured.

How it is checked

The test suite (testset 43) compares values with values:

  • the log-likelihood with one built from scratch over all NT observations, with log|det| of the full NT × NT Jacobian;
  • the analytic score and Hessian with automatic differentiation;
  • the pair matrices with the explicit T × T blocks of (W⊗I_T)C⁻¹ and (I_N⊗L_T)C⁻¹, and Σ_i g_i with the score;
  • Ω̂_M under a relabelling of the units;
  • E[Ω̂_M(α_0)] = Var(Σ_i g_i(α_0)), by simulation with skewed χ² errors and the data held fixed. Equations (52)–(53) make this exact in finite samples, so it is a null-distribution test of the variance. The plain outer product of the unit scores fails the same test.

Monte Carlo

validation/mc_serl_sdpd.jl runs Bai & Chen's long-running design (§6.2.1) at T = 5, ρ = 0.5, with 500 panels per N, each fitted at L = 0 and L = 1:

  • W is 8-nearest-neighbour;
  • the regressors are x_it = 0.5λ_i + √0.75 h_it, with h an AR(1);
  • y runs 100 periods before y_0.

Intervals are 95%. The Monte Carlo standard error of a coverage near .95 is about .010.

All 4000 fits converged except 3 at N = 500, L = 0. Those 3 threw errors rather than return a non-solution.

  • At L = 0, ρ̂ is inconsistent, as the paper finds. Its bias stays near −.014 while its sd falls from .018 to .007, so coverage falls from .82 to .40. The fall is slower than in the paper's Table 4 (.642 and .312 at N = 500 and 1000). Our network and factor path are drawn differently from theirs.

  • At L = 1, ρ̂ holds up to N = 4000: bias at most .006, coverage .93 to .96.

  • Coverage of β falls with N at both L. At N = 4000 it is .85 to .89, and SERL order does not explain it. validation/mc_serl_sdpd_diagnostics.jl separates the possible causes (200 panels each):

    • The standard errors are right. In the baseline design the model is correctly specified at L = 0, and mean SE / sd is 0.94 to 1.03 for all four parameters, with coverage .940 to .955. In the long-running design, SE / sd for β is close to 1 as well.
    • The problem is a bias in β̂, and L does not cause it. L = 2 gives the same bias as L = 1, to four decimals.
    • The cause is the default Q_X. Here the regressors' deviations from 0.5λ_i are an AR(1). The best predictor of λ_i from x_i1, …, x_iT then weights the periods unequally, and the unit mean leaves part of λ_i in η_i, correlated with X. Passing every period of x as Q reduces the bias to within its Monte Carlo error, about .0012.

The bias is fixed while the sd shrinks like 1/√N, so it matters only at large N. When the regressors may be serially correlated with the loadings, pass Q = hcat(X_1, …, X_T). This costs KT controls per spatial order instead of K.
